## Lesson 3

"I seek refuge in Allāh from Satan the accursed. By Allāh's Name, The Entirely Merciful, The Especially Merciful."

O Allah, bestow blessings upon our master Muḥammad, the opener of what had been closed and the seal of what had preceded, the helper of truth through truth, the guide to Your straight path, and upon his family, the true measure of his worth and his immense station. May Allah be pleased with the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, ṣ.a.w.s. There is no power and no strength except in Allah, the Most High, the Tremendous.

O aspiration (himma) of the Shaykh, draw near to us at this gathering; incline toward us with a glance that shall bring us to triumph.

(Indeed, Allah is not ashamed to strike a similitude — even that of a gnat or what is above it.) This verse was revealed in response to what the Jews said when Allah struck the similitude of the fly, in His saying (And if the fly should rob them of anything, they could not rescue it from it), and the similitude of the spider in Sūrat al-ʿAnkabūt, in His saying (like the spider). They said: What did Allah intend by mentioning these lowly things? It is not fitting that Allah, Blessed and Exalted, should strike similitudes with such contemptible things — it was His due to be ashamed of that. So the verse was revealed. This is the occasion of the revelation of this verse: Allah struck a similitude with these contemptible things, and the disbelievers, the hypocrites, and the Jews found it objectionable that a similitude of this kind should be struck concerning the Majestic, Blessed and Exalted. So Allah responded to them, saying (Indeed Allah is not ashamed) — that is, He does not refrain — (to strike) — to make — (a similitude, even that of a gnat or what is above it). Allah is not ashamed to give a similitude by means of the gnat, or by something lesser than it, or by something above it; He does not omit to make clear the wisdom contained therein. (As for those who believe, they know that it) — the similitude — (is the truth), established and situated exactly where it belongs,

(from their Lord). When a similitude of this kind is struck, people fall into two categories: the believers know that the similitude is situated exactly where it belongs; (but as for those who disbelieve, they say, What did Allah intend by this as a similitude?) — that is, by this similitude? He answers: (thereby He leads many) — by this similitude — (astray) from the truth, on account of their disbelief, (and thereby He guides many) of the believers, on account of their affirmation of it, (and thereby He leads astray none but the wicked transgressors).

He is not ashamed to strike a similitude. Shame (istiḥyāʾ) is the constriction a servant feels when doing something for which he would be reproached or blamed, such that he abandons it. This constriction, arising from aversion to being blamed, is impossible in respect of Allah, Blessed and Exalted. We stated to you as a principle that every attribute impossible of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, if you examine it closely, you will find has a beginning and an end: its beginning is impossible in respect of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, while its end is permissible in respect of Him. Shame is the constriction felt before a thing for which one would be blamed or reproached, such that one abandons it; the abandoning is the end-point, and abandonment is indeed permissible of Allah, Blessed and Exalted. Since a person of great standing is restrained from doing a given thing only by shame — for he fears no one, so nothing checks him but shame — and Allah, Blessed and Exalted, is greater than every great one, the term shame has been applied to Him in a figurative sense, the intended meaning being simply abandonment: that is, He does not abandon striking the similitude out of any shame.

The disbelievers who objected to the striking of a similitude with these contemptible things did so out of their obtuseness and the blindness of their hearts. For these contemptible things are among the strangest of what Allah, Blessed and Exalted, has created, and they contain wonders found in nothing else. The gnat, for instance, despite the smallness of its body, possesses every organ found in the elephant, and two wings besides. Allah, Blessed and Exalted, who had the power to create the elephant, also had the power to create the gnat and to fit into it organs and joints equal in number to those of the elephant; nothing is beyond His power. This small creature, the mosquito — when Allah created it weak, and likewise created the fly weak — He placed in it such boldness that it fears no one. When people gather, you find the fly hovering over their eyelids and their eyes, and however vigorously they drive it away, it returns to them, unconcerned; even kings, even tyrants, the mosquito alights upon them without fearing them.

As for the elephant and the lion, Allah created them at the utmost degree of strength and solidity, yet He placed within them such timidity that they do not dare draw near to people; they dwell in the wilderness, and if they hear the sound of a human being, they flee from him. This is among the subtle kindnesses (luṭf) of Allah, Blessed and Exalted: had He placed this boldness and daring found in contemptible creatures within these powerful ones, they would have harmed people, indeed destroyed them within a short time. Had the lion dared approach a human being as the fly and the mosquito dare, all people would have perished. But out of His kindness, Blessed and Exalted, He placed fear in the strong and boldness in the weak, which harms no one. Yet the fly, when it lands on you, spoils your life for you, whether you are in your house or your garden or wherever you may be; and the mosquito is likewise. Given the contemptibility, weakness, and helplessness of these creatures, how then can one dare to defy Allah, Blessed and Exalted, given the severity of His punishment and what is in Hellfire of scorpions and serpents whose harm is unceasing? Everyone who disobeys the command of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, and abandons what He has commanded, is like one who dares this severe punishment, while a mere flea or mosquito or fly is enough to spoil his life and trouble him. Let him know, then, that he cannot bear anything of the punishment of Allah, Blessed and Exalted.

And despite their contemptibility, these things do not prevent the similitude from being exactly fitting: (O mankind, a similitude has been struck, so listen to it: those you call upon besides Allah will never create a fly) — this is of the utmost eloquence. And He struck a similitude for the worship of idols: (their likeness is as the likeness of the spider that takes to itself a house, and truly the frailest of houses is the house of the spider). The striking of similitudes with such things also occurred in the scriptures revealed before the Qurʾān.

Al-Maʾmūn was once upon his pulpit when a fly landed on him; he drove it away, and it landed on him again repeatedly, and however he drove it away he could do nothing to prevent it. So he sent for Abū Hudhayl, a scholar in the city, to ask him what wisdom lay in Allah's creation of these things. Abū Hudhayl said to him: Allah created these things to humble tyrants such as yourself, for when the tyrannical king sits upon his throne, the fly lands upon his excrement and then lands upon him, and he is powerless to keep it away. Al-Maʾmūn said to him: You have spoken truly; it is for this that Allah, Blessed and Exalted, created it.

It is said, too, that were it not for the winds and the flies, the world would putrefy — for these foul odors are consumed by the flies, and the winds carry them away so that they dissipate; were it not for that, the world would putrefy and harm its inhabitants. He said, (thereby He leads many astray and thereby He guides many). How can He, Glorified is His Majesty, say that He guides many, when the people of guidance are few in relation to the people of misguidance? The people of misguidance are many and the people of guidance are few; yet despite their fewness, they are many in themselves — not in relation to the people of misguidance, but many in themselves. Or: the people of truth, even if few, are the vast majority in weight, the many, the prevailing ones, and it is by them that the standard is set. But in reality, those who go astray are more numerous than those who are guided, and this is due to the rarity of true faith and to Allah's utter independence of His creation, Blessed and Exalted — had He willed, He would have caused everyone on earth, all together, to believe: (And had your Lord willed, everyone on earth, all together, would have believed). But because of His independence from creation, He made most of the jinn and mankind people of misguidance. (And thereby He leads astray none but the wicked transgressors) —

those who go outside obedience to Him — (those who break the covenant of Allah), that which He entrusted to them in the scriptures concerning belief in Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s. Allah's covenant is threefold: the first is that which He took from the progeny of Adam on the day He said, (Am I not your Lord?) and they said, (Yes indeed); the second is that which He took from the prophets, that if they should live to see the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., they would believe in him and follow him; and the third is that which He took from the scholars: (You shall make it clear to mankind and not conceal it). This covenant the Jews broke. He described the covenant as broken as though He had made it a rope, for the covenant is called a rope in the speech of the Arabs, and this is why it is permissible to speak of its being broken. (after its solemn confirmation) — after its being made firm — (and sever what Allah has commanded to be joined), namely belief in the Prophet, the bonds of kinship, and other things, (and work corruption in the earth), through acts of disobedience and obstruction of belief — (those are the losers), because of their destiny to the Fire made everlasting upon them.

(How can you disbelieve) — O people of Mecca — (in Allah, when you were) — indeed you were — (dead), mere sperm-drops within the loins, (and He gave you life) — in the wombs, in this world, by breathing the spirit into you — the interrogative here expresses astonishment: how can they disbelieve after all this? Not at all! (Then He shall cause you to die), when your appointed terms come to an end, (then He shall give you life), through resurrection, (then to Him you shall be returned) — you shall be brought back after the resurrection, and He will requite you for your deeds.

And He offered a proof of the resurrection, since they denied it: (It is He who created for you all that is in the earth) — the earth and everything in it — (altogether), that you might benefit from it and take heed. The Truth, Blessed and Exalted, informed us that He created the earth and everything in it for the children of Adam, all of it, so that they might benefit from it and take heed that the Creator who has the power to do this has the power to resurrect them after their death. Here the lām indicates entitlement — that is, it was the children of Adam who came to possess the earth and what is in it — it does not indicate unrestricted permissibility. This is the error into which the Ibāḥiyya fell, a doctrine among the doctrines of the Muʿtazila, who say that Allah created everything in the earth for the children of Adam, so that nothing on the face of the earth is forbidden to us once Allah has created it for us. But this is not what is intended.

(Then He turned) — after creating the earth, He directed His purpose — (to the heaven and levelled them), that is, He made them — (seven heavens, and He has knowledge of all things), knowing everything, in its totality and in its detail. Do you not then take heed that the One who has the power to create this in the first instance, which is far greater than you, has the power to bring you back again? In this verse, (He created for you all that is in the earth, altogether; then He turned to the heaven), it seems that the creation of the heaven came after the creation of the earth, whereas in Sūrat al-Nāziʿāt it is said, (and the earth, after that, He spread out), which seems to indicate that the creation of the earth came after that of the heavens. The reconciliation between the two is that Allah, Blessed and Exalted, created the earth first, then created the heavens, and after creating the heavens, He spread out the earth and made it as it is now — so each of the two is, in a sense, both prior and posterior to the other.

And, mention, O Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s., (when your Lord said to the angels, Indeed I am placing a vicegerent (khalīfa) on the earth) — one who will succeed Me in carrying out My rulings therein. Allah, Blessed and Exalted, related that He informed the angels that He was placing a vicegerent on earth, and this was Adam. (They said, Will You place therein one who will work corruption therein) — through acts of disobedience — (and shed blood) — spill it through killing, just as the sons of the jinn had done, for they existed before Adam and used to work corruption in the earth and shed blood — will You place a vicegerent like them? (While we glorify You with praise) — engaged in praising You, saying, Glory be to Allah and praise be to Him — (and sanctify You), declaring You exalted above whatever does not befit You. (He said, Indeed I know what you do not know) — of the benefit contained in making Adam a vicegerent, and that among his progeny would be both the obedient and the disobedient, so that justice would become manifest among them. And they had said: our Lord will never create a creature more honored in His sight than us, nor more knowing, since we preceded him and have seen what he has not seen. So Allah, exalted is He, created Adam from the surface of the earth — that is, its face — taking a handful from all its various colors, which was kneaded with different waters, then fashioned him and breathed into him the spirit, so that he became a sentient living being after having been inanimate matter. This is the story of Adam's vicegerency.

Allah said, (Indeed I am placing a vicegerent on the earth) — this is Adam. Allah, Blessed and Exalted, made him His vicegerent in existence, through whom all His rulings and works are carried out — like the seal by which every king safeguards his kingdom. And Allah, Blessed and Exalted, mentioned that when He created Adam, He created him as a human being; when He spoke of him as a human being, He said, (Indeed I am creating a human being), whereas here, when speaking of him and making him a vicegerent, He said placing, not creating — for in his capacity as vicegerent, he does not belong to the created realm (ʿālam al-khalq) but rather to the realm of command (ʿālam al-amr).

He informed the angels that Adam and his progeny would be Allah's vicegerents on earth, and the angels responded with this reply, (Will You place therein one who will work corruption therein and shed blood?) From where did they know that the progeny of Adam would work corruption in the earth and shed blood? They knew it from the very constitution of Adam's creation. Allah created Adam and placed within him a soul possessed of desire (shahwa) and anger (ghaḍab), and also reason (ʿaql). The human being has three faculties: the faculty of anger, which sheds blood; the faculty of desire, which works corruption in the earth through sins; and the faculty of reason, which sets things right. So the angels looked at Adam and said that these two faculties would inevitably overpower the single remaining faculty, and thus corruption and bloodshed would inevitably occur.

It is also said that they understood that Adam and his progeny would work corruption in the earth from the very word vicegerency (khilāfa) itself, for khilāfa is derived from khilāf, meaning contravention — every vicegerent must inevitably contravene the one before him. Since Adam was to be Allah's vicegerent, they understood that corruption and bloodshed would necessarily proceed from him. Or, they read this in the Preserved Tablet, for it was written there that Adam would be a vicegerent on earth and would have progeny, some of whom would work corruption in the earth and shed blood until Allah filled Hell with them, and some of whom would be obedient, and Allah would fill Paradise with them; they read this in the heavenly books, and this is why they said what they said. Or, they had heard this from Allah, Blessed and Exalted, previously, when He addressed them privately beforehand, and they said to Him: O our God, this Fire is a creation of tremendous power — what will You do with it? He said to them: I shall create a creature and bring forth progeny from his loins; among them will be those who obey Me, and I shall admit them into Paradise, and among them will be those who disobey Me, and I shall admit them into the Fire. Their objection to Adam's vicegerency, then, they understood from these various considerations.

(While we glorify You with praise and sanctify You. He said, Indeed I know what you do not know.) So Allah, Blessed and Exalted, turned away from them, (and He taught Adam the names) — the names of the things named, that is, the names of all created beings — (all of them), down to the large bowl and the small bowl, the small emission of wind and its diminutive, and the ladle. Allah, Blessed and Exalted, taught him everything that He would create: this one's name is horse, and the wisdom in it is such-and-such; this one's name is donkey, and this... down to the large bowl and the small bowl, the large vessel and the small one, and the ladle — Adam came to know every single thing at once, from the beginning of time to its end, while the angels knew nothing of this.

(Then He presented them to the angels and said), in reproach, (Inform Me) — tell Me — (of the names of these), the things named, (if you speak truly) — that is, in claiming that I would not create anyone more knowing than you, or that you are more deserving of the vicegerency than Adam. (They said, Glory be to You!) — declaring You exalted above any objection to You — (we have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, You are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise), from whose knowledge and wisdom nothing escapes.

(He), exalted is He, (said, Adam, inform them) — the angels — (of their names), the things named. Adam named every single thing by its name and mentioned the wisdom for which it had been created. (And when he had informed them of their names, He), exalted is He, (said to them), in rebuke, (Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you disclose) — what you revealed in your saying, Will You place therein... — (and what you were concealing) — what you were keeping secret, namely your saying that Allah would never create anyone more honored in His sight than you, nor more knowing.

This is the story of Adam with the angels. When Allah created Adam, He taught him these things, which the angels did not know, and so Allah argued against them that Adam deserved the vicegerency because he possessed knowledge which the angels did not possess. This is why the scholars have said that leadership (imāma) is deserved only by the one who possesses knowledge — for Allah, Blessed and Exalted, argued against the angels concerning Adam's vicegerency by means of knowledge. This is the merit of knowledge (ʿilm). Because Adam knew the names of things and the angels did not know them, Allah made him a vicegerent on earth and commanded the angels — as will shortly be related — to prostrate to him in a prostration of greeting. This is proof of the nobility of knowledge: knowledge is the leader, and action follows it. The merit of knowledge is established by the Book, the Sunna, and reason.

As for the Book, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, mentioned the merit of knowledge in a number of verses, among them: (It is only those of His servants who possess knowledge that truly fear Allah), and among them: (Allah bears witness that there is no god but He, and the angels, and those possessed of knowledge). And Allah mentioned four categories of people whose ranks He raises: the people of Badr — (The believers are only those who, when Allah is mentioned, their hearts tremble), this having been revealed concerning the people of Badr, and He said at the end that He raises their ranks, granting them lofty degrees; and He mentioned those who strive (mujāhidūn) — (Allah has preferred those who strive above those who sit back, by a tremendous reward, by degrees); and He raised, Blessed and Exalted, the ranks of the righteous — (and whoever comes to Him as a believer, having done righteous deeds — those shall have the highest ranks); and He raised the ranks of the people of knowledge above all of these categories — (Allah raises those of you who believe, and those who have been given knowledge, in ranks) — placing those possessed of knowledge in the third rank: (Allah bears witness that there is no god but He, and the angels, and those possessed of knowledge).

In the Qurʾān there are seven individuals of whom Allah mentions that He taught them knowledge directly from Himself, without intermediary: (He taught Adam the names, all of them), and by this he attained the vicegerency and the prostration of the angels to him; and He, exalted is He, said that He taught al-Khiḍr the knowledge of insight (firāsa): (and We had taught him knowledge from Our presence), and by this he gained a disciple like Moses and Joshua; and He taught Joseph the interpretation of dreams: (My Lord, You have given me of sovereignty and taught me the interpretation of narratives), and by this he attained Zulaykhā and the kingdom; and He taught David the craft of making coats of mail: (and We taught him the making of coats of mail), and by this he attained leadership and kingship; and He taught Solomon the speech of birds: (we have been taught the speech of birds) — and the Teacher was Allah — and by this he attained the kingdom, and Bilqīs, and the Throne; and He taught Jesus the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel: (and He will teach him the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel), and by this he attained being raised up to heaven and fellowship with the angels; and He taught Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s., the sacred law: (and He taught you what you did not know), and by his knowledge he attained the power of intercession.

ʿAlī, may Allah ennoble his countenance, said: Knowledge is superior to wealth in seven respects:
– Wealth is the inheritance of the Pharaohs, while knowledge is the inheritance of the prophets.
– Wealth diminishes through spending, while knowledge does not diminish through spending.
– Wealth is possessed by the disbeliever and the believer alike, while knowledge is possessed only by a believer.
– Wealth requires a guardian and a protector, while knowledge protects its possessor and requires no protector.
– When a person dies, his wealth remains behind, but his knowledge enters the grave together with him and does not leave him.
– Wealth weighs a person down on the Bridge (ṣirāṭ), while knowledge strengthens him to walk upon the Bridge.
– All people are in need of knowledge for the sake of their religion, but they are not in need of the possessor of wealth.

O Kumayl, knowledge is better than wealth: knowledge guards you, while you must guard wealth; knowledge governs, while wealth is governed; wealth is diminished by spending, while knowledge grows through being expended.

Knowledge, then, is superior to wealth. And if Adam, upon him be peace, was taught the names of created things, and was ennobled by that knowledge to the point that he received the prostration and greeting of the angels, then we have been taught the Essence, the Attributes, and the Names — not the names of created things, but rather the Names of Allah, His Attributes, and His exalted Essence. Once we know this, how could we fail to attain the greeting of the angels? Indeed, we attain something beyond that: the greeting of the Lord Himself — (Peace! — a word from a Merciful Lord). And if al-Khiḍr, through the knowledge of insight, attained the companionship of Moses and Joshua, two prophets, then we, having attained the knowledge of ultimate reality (ʿilm al-ḥaqīqa), how could we fail to attain the companionship of Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s.? We do attain it: (those are with the ones whom Allah has favored, of the prophets, the truthful ones, the martyrs, and the righteous — and excellent are those as companions). Praise be to Allah.

Among the merits of knowledge is that when the student leaves his house for a scholar in pursuit of knowledge, the angels spread their wings for him in honor and veneration; and as long as he continues going to his teacher, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, sends mercy down upon him; and while he is in a gathering of knowledge, the one who understands the knowledge knows what benefit he attains, while the one who understands nothing of what is said still attains blessings — he attains the fact that he is in obedience to Allah, Blessed and Exalted, as long as he remains in the gathering of knowledge, even if he understands nothing; he is thereby restrained from sins and transgressions as long as he remains in that gathering, and he sits in the company of scholars and angels. And if he understands nothing of what is said, his heart is humbled on that account, and so Allah becomes his companion: (I am with those whose hearts are humbled for My sake). This is something of the merit of knowledge.

When Allah wished to alert the angels that Adam was better than them, He gave him of knowledge what they did not possess, and so they acknowledged that Adam was better than them.

And, mention, O Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s., (when We said to the angels, Prostrate to Adam) — a prostration of greeting, involving a bow, or as though it were a bow of salutation, not a prostration of worship, which is due to none but Allah, Blessed and Exalted. Or, Adam was made a direction (qibla) for them, just as the Kaʿba was made a direction for us: we prostrate toward it, not to it in reality. In any case, this prostration to other than Allah, Blessed and Exalted, has been abrogated. (So they prostrated, except Iblīs) — he is the father of the jinn, who was among the angels — (he refused), declining to prostrate, (and was proud), saying, I am better than he, (and he became one of the disbelievers). Iblīs used to be called ʿAzāzīl; he was a servant brought near to Allah, having worshiped Allah, Blessed and Exalted, upon every handspan of the face of the earth for a thousand years, and he had been treasurer of Paradise for forty thousand years, and had striven in worship for sixty thousand years — all of this before Adam. When Allah created Adam and commanded the angels to prostrate to him, Iblīs reasoned by analogy (qiyās) despite the presence of the explicit text, saying that he was better than Adam and so would not prostrate to him. He refused, while the angels prostrated.

The prostration was originally a single act, and this was what analogy required and what was fitting, since Allah is One, so the prostration was one. But when the angels had prostrated for half a day — five hundred years — and raised their heads from the prostration, and saw that Iblīs had refused and had not prostrated, they returned to prostration a second time, prostrating a second prostration in gratitude to Allah, Blessed and Exalted, that He had not tried them with what He had tried Iblīs. Thus the prostration became twofold: there was one bowing (rukūʿ) and one prostration (sujūd), because the One worshiped is one, but the second prostration was an act of thanksgiving to Allah, Blessed and Exalted.

## Lesson 4

And [mention] when Moses sought water for his people — that is, recall, O Muhammad, when he entreated for water on their behalf, al-istisqāʾ meaning to ask for water for one's people — for they had grown thirsty in the wilderness. We said, "Strike the rock with your staff" — that is, the very rock that had once fled with his garment, a light, four-sided stone the size of a man's head, of marble or soft limestone. He struck it, and it burst open and streamed forth: from it gushed twelve springs, equal to the number of the tribes, so that every group — each tribe among them — came to know its own drinking-place, which it shared with none of the others. And We said to them, "Eat of the provision of God, and drink of God's provision, and do not act wickedly in the earth, working corruption" — corruption here meaning to spread ruin, the phrase "working corruption" being a circumstantial qualifier reinforcing the verb it governs. The Exalted informs us that when Moses, upon him be peace, was in the wilderness with the Children of Israel, they grew thirsty and asked him to pray for water on their behalf. Herein lies the difference between the Children of Israel and the umma (community) of Muhammad, ṣ.a.w.s.: the nations before us had no right to ask anything of God, the Blessed and Exalted, directly. Whenever they wished to make any request whatsoever, they would bring it to the prophet of their age, for it was he alone who petitioned God, the Blessed and Exalted, directly — as is shown by Moses's praying for water on his people's behalf.

"And when you said, O Moses, we cannot endure one kind of food; pray to your Lord for us, that He may bring forth for us of what the earth produces" — none of this could they ask for directly from God, the Blessed and Exalted, Himself. This umma, however, God has granted the absolute license to supplicate: He has said, "And ask of God from His bounty," and He has said, "Call upon Me and I will answer you." Whoever among us is faced with any task or need simply asks God, the Blessed and Exalted, and He answers him.

They asked Moses, upon him be prayer and peace, for water. Now Moses was a man of great modesty (sitr), possessed of manly virtue (murūʾa) and shyness among his people. His people used to strip themselves bare when washing away seminal impurity, so that some of them would see the nakedness of others, but Moses, from the time he grew up, refused to do so: whenever he wished to wash, he would go far off, conceal himself, and wash alone. So they said, "Nothing drives Moses to this except that he has some concealed defect." One day, when he had gone apart, washed, and come out of the water, he wished to take up his garment, and the rock ran off with it. He went about crying, "My garment, O rock! My garment, O rock!" — meaning, O rock, give me back my garment — until he came before an assembly of people, who saw that there was nothing amiss with him. He then took his garment, put it on, and — being angry — took up his staff and struck the rock, whereupon God revealed to him that he was to carry that rock with him and never part from it. So when they were in the wilderness and asked him for water, God revealed to him that he should strike this rock with his staff. He struck it — it being four-sided, with four corners, each corner producing three springs — and twelve springs came forth in all, corresponding to the twelve tribes of the Children of Israel, as God, the Blessed and Exalted, has said; each tribe had its own separate drinking-place. This is the meaning of "each group knew its drinking-place."

The Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., was likewise asked by his people for water. He ascended the pulpit, raised his hands with their backs turned toward the sky, and said, "O God, water Your land, have mercy upon Your servants, spread abroad Your mercy, and revive Your dead land." The narrator relates that the sky was like glass, without a single wisp of cloud in it, when a cloud came and enveloped the city, and rain fell from that Friday until the following Friday.

Now, Moses's people had asked Moses to pray to God for water on their behalf, and Moses prayed at his people's request, and God answered his prayer. How much more fitting, then, that Muhammad, ṣ.a.w.s., who prayed for us at the very command of God, the Blessed and Exalted — as in His words, "and ask forgiveness for your sin, and for the believing men and believing women" — this being a command from God that the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., seek forgiveness on our behalf. He asks forgiveness for us by God's own command. Since Moses's prayer was answered though he prayed only at his people's request, then Muhammad's prayer — offered at the command of God, the Blessed and Exalted, Himself — is all the stronger and more to be hoped for. We therefore have great hope in the fact that God commanded His Prophet to pray to Him for us and to seek forgiveness on our behalf.

"Eat and drink of God's provision": He commanded them to eat freely and to drink freely, and this might well have carried them into indulgence and excess leading to corruption, so He followed it directly with His words, "and do not act wickedly in the earth, working corruption" — eat and drink, but do not act wickedly in the earth, working corruption; and elsewhere again, "Eat and drink, but do not be excessive; verily He does not love the excessive." Just as Moses asked for water on behalf of his people, the Children of Israel, and twelve springs burst forth from the rock, so too does Moses the spirit (rūḥ) within every human being ask for water on behalf of his own people, who are the five outward senses and the five inward senses, together with the lower self (nafs) and the heart (qalb) — twelve faculties in all. So when the rock of the servant's existence is struck with the staff of lā ilāha illā Llāh (there is no god but God), twelve springs burst forth, and each group knows its drinking-place: the drinking-place of the nafs is worship and spiritual struggle (mujāhada); the drinking-place of the qalb is fear and hope; the drinking-place of the ʿaql (intellect) is contraction and expansion; the drinking-place of the rūḥ is the witnessing of Beauty and Majesty; and the drinking-place of the sirr (innermost secret) is intimacy and awe — as it is said, "and their Lord will give them to drink a pure drink." "Eat and drink of God's provision, and do not act wickedly in the earth, working corruption" — that is, by reverting to the appetites of the lower self and disobeying the command.

Moses struck a rock and twelve springs burst forth from it; yet more astonishing still is what the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., showed us, for water bursting forth from stone may sometimes be witnessed in certain places, whereas the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., caused water to spring forth from between his own noble fingers — and this has never been witnessed at all. This practice (sunna) of the prayer for rain remains with us to this day, whether the need be for crops, for livestock, or for people's drinking-water: the imam and the Muslims with him go out in a state of humility and abasement and perform the prayer of istisqāʾ, and God answers them.

Moses once asked for water on his people's behalf, and he remained a long while praying and supplicating, yet he was not answered. He said, "O Lord, we call upon You — more than three thousand of the righteous, all in need of what You have created — and yet You do not answer us." God said, "I have not answered you because among you is a talebearer (nammām), one who spreads slander among the people." Moses said, "O Lord, make him known to me." God said, "Were I to make him known to you, I would become like him myself, exposing a fault in him and doing the very thing I forbid — this would not be fitting." The next day, God revealed to Moses that the talebearer had died and that his bier was at the door of the mosque. Moses hastened to reach the funeral procession and learn who it was, but he found seventy biers there, God, the Blessed and Exalted, having concealed him.

"And when you said, O Moses, we cannot endure one kind of food." Likewise the Children of Israel in the wilderness, having grown weary of that life, claimed that they had grown weary of the food that descended from heaven, which was of a single kind, manna and quails. They lied: they had not grown weary of the food; they had grown weary only of that life of wandering. "Pray to your Lord for us, that He may bring forth for us something of what the earth produces — its herbs, its cucumbers, its garlic, its lentils, and its onions." These are things that grow only from tilled soil; they knew well that this was possible only if they returned to villages and towns, and this indeed was their true aim. Moses said to them, "Would you exchange what is better for what is meaner?" — that is, would you take the lesser in place of the more excellent? They said, "We want nothing but this." So Moses prayed to God, the Blessed and Exalted, and He, Exalted is He, said, "Go down to a city" — some city or other — "for you shall have what you asked for," of produce. So they went down to Jerusalem, or to Jericho, a town near Jerusalem, and they cultivated the land, and God provided them with these plants, and this remains their livelihood to this very day. "And abasement and wretchedness were stamped upon them" — dhilla being disgrace and humiliation, and maskana the mark of poverty, submissiveness, and ignominy: these are inseparable from them even when they are wealthy, just as a struck coin never loses the mark of its mint. You will never see a Jew but that you see in him the traces of poverty, abasement, and wretchedness, even if he is rich. "And they incurred anger from God" — that affliction was because they used to disbelieve the signs of God and slay the prophets — such as Zachariah and John — "without right," that is, unjustly, for the slaying of prophets can never be anything but unjust. In a single day they slew seventy prophets, and when three hundred of the righteous rose up to rebuke them for it, they slew these as well. "That was because they disobeyed and were transgressors" — that is, they overstepped the bounds in disobedience. This is part of what God tells us concerning their forebears, whom they used to praise as having been upright, yet this Qurʾān lays bare the corruption of their forefathers.

"Surely those who believe" — in the prophets — "and those of Jewry" — that is, the Jews — "and the Christians" — "and the Sabaeans" — a community between the Jews and the Christians — "whoever among them believes" — in God and the Last Day, in the time of our Prophet — "and does righteous deeds" — according to the sharīʿa of Muhammad, ṣ.a.w.s. — "for them is their reward," the recompense of their deeds, "with their Lord, and no fear shall come upon them, nor shall they grieve." Whoever believes in the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., and holds fast to his sharīʿa is among the Muslims and among the friends of God (awliyāʾ Allāh), who shall have no fear, nor shall they grieve. There is no difference here between one origin and another, whether a person's origin be Christian, Jewish, Sabaean, or Zoroastrian: if he testifies to the shahāda and acts according to the sharīʿa of Muhammad, he is among the righteous servants of God.

And remember when We took your covenant and raised the Mount above you — We uprooted it from its very base when you refused to accept it — and We said, "Take hold, with strength, of what We have given you" — with diligence and effort — "and remember what is in it," that is, act according to what is in it, "that you may guard yourselves against evil." When Moses brought the Torah, containing as it did the obligations of command and prohibition, the licit and the illicit, the Children of Israel refused to act according to it. So Gabriel took hold of a mountain the size of their encampment — they numbered six hundred thousand — uprooting a mountain to their measure and raising it above them, declaring that if they did not act according to the Torah as God had commanded, the mountain would fall upon them and they would all perish. "Then you turned away after that" — they had accepted it only under duress, and a human being who accepts something under compulsion reverts to refusal whenever he is able to do so; having accepted it under duress, they apostatized from it afterward. "But for the bounty of God upon you, and His mercy" — in granting you repentance — "you would have been among the losers," the ruined. "And well you knew those among you who transgressed the Sabbath" — this too is among the corrupt deeds of their forefathers, whom they claimed had been righteous; among their forefathers also were the people of Eilat, in the time of David, upon him be peace, who transgressed the Sabbath, overstepping the bounds by fishing, which We had forbidden them. "We said to them, Be apes, despised" — so God destroyed them and turned them all into apes and swine, driven away. There had been one group that forbade the fishing and one group that fished: God destroyed the group that fished, as will be related in Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, and delivered the group that had forbidden it; and the Qurʾān is silent concerning a third group. It is said that they perished together with those who perished, and it is said that they were delivered together with those who were delivered; but we understand from the Qurʾān that they perished with those who perished, for it is said of them, "they used not to forbid one another the wrongdoing they committed — evil indeed was what they used to do." God said to them, "Be apes, despised" — this being a command of contempt (taḥqīr) from God, the Blessed and Exalted.

The imperative form (fiʿl al-amr) in the Qurʾān occurs in fifteen distinct senses; this is the underlying principle, for not every imperative denotes strict obligation. The basic sense of the imperative is obligation (wujūb), yet in the Qurʾān it occurs in fifteen senses. There is the imperative of obligation, which is the basic sense, as in "Establish the prayer," the establishment of prayer being obligatory. There is the imperative of recommendation (nadb), as in "Write it down for them if you know some good in them," this being recommended. There is the imperative of permissibility (ibāḥa), as in "Go and hunt," which does not make hunting obligatory but merely permits it to us. There is the imperative of guidance (irshād), as when you say to your young child, "Eat from what is nearest to you," this being simple instruction for him. There is the imperative of calling to witness (ishhād), as in "and call witnesses when you make a contract of sale," which is not obligatory but is simply a matter of bearing witness. There is the imperative of contempt (taḥqīr), as in "Be apes, despised," which reduces them to the very species of apes. There is the imperative of degradation (ihāna), as in "Be stones or iron," meaning, I care nothing for you — a humiliation of them. There is the imperative of equivalence (taswiya), as in "Bear it patiently or do not bear it patiently, it is the same for you," patience and impatience being equal here. There is the imperative of warning (indhār), as in "Eat and enjoy yourselves for a while." There is the imperative of threat (tahdīd), as in "Do as you wish," meaning, you will perish. There is the imperative of wishing (tamannī), as the poet said, "O long night, will you not clear" — a mere wish. There is the imperative of supplication (duʿāʾ), as in "My Lord, forgive me," this being a request made of God, the Blessed and Exalted. And there is the imperative of honoring (ikrām), as in "Enter it in peace, secure," this imperative containing within it an honoring of the one addressed. All this makes clear that not everything God commands is thereby rendered obligatory: it may be obligatory, or recommended, or a matter of guidance, of threat, or of simple permissibility.

"And when you slew a soul and disputed among yourselves concerning it, and God brought forth what you had concealed." The commentators place this account before the narration of the wickedness of the Children of Israel: they had killed a man of theirs named ʿĀmīl, a wealthy man of great age, whose kinsmen grew impatient for him to die so that they might inherit from him; and when his death was delayed, they grew impatient and killed him. They took him by night, went off with him, slaughtered him, and cast him down beside a neighboring quarter; then in the morning they went about tracing his fate, until they came to that quarter and accused its people of having killed him. The people of the quarter denied it, so both parties came to Moses, upon him be prayer and peace, and asked him to reveal to them who had killed their slain kinsman. They said, "We ask you to pray to God that He may reveal to us his killer." Moses said to them, "God commands you to sacrifice a cow" — that is, to slaughter a cow. God said, "And when Moses said to his people, God commands you to sacrifice a cow, they said, Do you take us in mockery?" — We ask you to reveal to us the killer of our slain kinsman, and you command us to slaughter a cow! "He said, I take refuge" — I seek protection — "in God, lest I be among the ignorant" — the mockers, for none mocks save one who is ignorant, and I seek refuge in God from ignorance. When they realized that this was Moses's firm resolve — and had they taken any cow at all and slaughtered it, they would have found what they sought — they made it difficult for themselves, and so God made it difficult for them. "They said, Pray to your Lord for us, that He make clear to us what she is" — that is, her age. Moses said, "He" — God — "says she is a cow neither old nor virgin."

"But of a middling age between the two" — so do as you are commanded regarding her slaughter. They pressed further still, saying, "Pray to your Lord for us, that He make clear to us her colour." He said, "He says she is a bright yellow cow, intensely yellow, pleasing to those who behold her" — that is, delighting them by her beauty. They said, "Pray to your Lord for us, that He make clear to us what she is: does she graze freely, or is she put to labour? The cows" — that is, cattle of this kind — "are all alike to us," on account of their great number, so that we have not been guided to the one intended; "but if God wills, we shall be guided" to her. In the ḥadīth it is related: "Had they not made that exception, it would never have been made plain to them, ever" — for when they said, "if God wills," by the blessing of that phrase the answer then came, and they were guided to what was sought. He said, "He says she is a cow not broken," not tamed for labour "to plough the earth," turning it over for cultivation, "or to water the tillage," the land prepared for sowing — "sound," free of blemish and the marks of toil, "with no" other colour "upon her." They said, "Now you have brought the truth" — that is, now you have made clear to us what you meant. So they sought her out and found her in the possession of a youth most dutiful to his mother, and they bought her for the equivalent of her hide filled with gold.

There had once been a righteous man who died, leaving behind a young son and nothing else but a cow. He went to the forest and said, "O Lord of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, I entrust to You this cow of mine; when my son comes of age, deliver it to him." The boy grew up righteous and dutiful to his mother: when day came he would gather firewood, and when he brought the firewood he would sell it and divide its price into three portions — one portion for himself to spend, one he would give to his mother, and one he would give in charity; and when night came he would divide his time likewise into three portions — one for sleep, one to sit with his mother, and one to devote to the worship of God, the Blessed and Exalted — until his mother, moved with tenderness for him, said to him, "Go to the forest, for your father entrusted a cow for you there to the Lord of Abraham. When you come to the forest, say, O Lord of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, deliver to me my cow." So when he came to the forest he said this, and the cow came to him and said, "Ride me." He said, "No — my mother did not command me to ride you."

## Lesson 5

The occasion of revelation for this verse is that the Jews and the polytheists impugned the abrogation (naskh) of certain verses of the Qurʾān, saying, "If this were truly the speech of Allāh, it would not be abrogated, for abrogation cannot occur in the speech of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He." This objection stemmed from their extreme ignorance, for abrogation did indeed occur in the Qurʾān, established both by the transmitted text and by reason. Abrogation takes three forms. First, a verse whose recitation and ruling were both abrogated together, such as His saying, "Ten known sucklings render [marriage] forbidden" — this had been a verse that was recited and whose ruling was in force, and then both its recitation and its ruling were abrogated. Second, a verse whose recitation was abrogated while its ruling remained in force, such as His saying, "The married man and the married woman, if they commit fornication, stone them absolutely" — this had been a verse that was recited; its recitation was lifted while its ruling continued in force. Third, a verse whose ruling was lifted while its recitation remained, such as His saying, "It is prescribed for you, when death approaches one of you, if he leaves behind goods..." [Q. 2:180], which was abrogated by the verse of inheritance and by the saying of the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s.: "There is no bequest to an heir." The verse was not abrogated by the ḥadīth; rather, the ḥadīth made clear that the verse had been abrogated. For the verse itself declared that whatever Allāh abrogates, He replaces with something better, and the speech of a created being can never surpass the speech of the Creator — except that revelation is of two kinds: that which is recited, and that which is sent down upon the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., for him to explain to us. In any case, abrogation is established in the Qurʾān by the transmitted text; and rationally, because Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, is the Agent who chooses freely and disposes of existence as He wills. He may let a ruling run for a time and then, if He wills, bring it to an end and replace it with a new ruling, and none may object to this. It was in this connection that this verse was revealed.

"Whatever revelation We abrogate" — meaning We lift its ruling, either together with its wording or its ruling alone — "or cause to be forgotten" — meaning We defer its recitation without lifting its ruling, so that its ruling remains established even though its recitation has been suspended — "We bring one better than it" — meaning of greater benefit to [Allāh's] servants, whether through ease or abundance of reward — "or similar to it" — in obligation, in reward, and in the conclusiveness of its proof. "Do you not know that Allāh has power over all things?" — abrogation and substitution being among those things; the interrogative here serves as an affirmation, not a genuine question. "Do you not know that to Allāh belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth?" — He is the Owner who disposes as He wills, granted that they acknowledge He is the One [without partner].

Abrogation, moreover, may take several further forms. It may be that both the wording and the ruling of a verse are lifted once the underlying cause has lapsed, as with the verse, "If any of your wives should escape to the disbelievers..." — a ruling that lapsed once its occasion lapsed. Or it may be that the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., performs an act, or commands his community to perform one, that is not itself the wording of a ruling from the Book of Allāh, Mighty and Majestic is He, and Allāh then abrogates it by a ruling He sends down in His Book — as when the prayer toward Jerusalem was abrogated, and when he was forbidden to seek forgiveness for his uncle. Or scholars may differ over two verses as to which of the two abrogates the other, or whether neither abrogates the other; yet even where they agree to treat the disputed verse as abrogated or not, this is done only provisionally and out of caution, not with certainty — as with His saying, "...and that you take in marriage two sisters together..." and then His saying, "except what your right hands possess," one verse permitting what the other forbids. Or scholars may differ over whether one of two verses abrogates the other, yet agree in establishing its ruling under two different senses, as with His saying, "Neither be loud in your prayer, nor silent in it" [Q. 17:110] — meaning: do not lower your voice so far that your companions cannot learn [the Qurʾān] from you, yet do not raise it so loud that the polytheists revile the Qurʾān.

He is the All-Compelling (al-Qahhār), who does what He wills; none may object to Him in the abrogation of some of His rulings. "And you have, besides Allāh, no protector and no helper" — none who can avert His punishment from you, should it come upon you. Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, had already made clear, in the last verse we read yesterday, the ruling on magic (siḥr): magic is unlawful, a grave sin and disbelief (kufr); it is wholly incompatible with faith, and none practises it save a disbeliever. The proof that magic is disbelief is that Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, exonerated Solomon of it, saying, "Solomon did not disbelieve" — meaning, he did not practise magic. Magic being disbelief on this account, the angels whom Allāh sent down came in order to teach magic — that is, to teach that it is an evil, so that people would shun it. The exegetes relate a story about these two angels, Hārūt and Mārūt, but the story has no sound basis; many exegetes have nevertheless repeated it, taking it only from Isrāʾīliyyāt [material borrowed from Jewish sources], and the Qurʾān itself gives it the lie.

The story, as commonly told, runs thus: the angels reproached the children of Adam for the abundance of their sins, whereupon Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, said, "I created them and instilled in them a desire that drives them to these transgressions; had I instilled that same desire in you, the like of what befell them would befall you." They replied, "It could never happen that we should disobey You." No angel, however, could truthfully give such an answer, so Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, said, "Choose, then, from among your most righteous," and they chose Hārūt and Mārūt, two of the most righteous of the angels. Allāh instilled in them the desire for food, drink, and intercourse, and sent them down to the earth, commanding them not to associate anything with Him, not to kill any soul, not to commit fornication, to judge only by what Allāh had sent down, and not to drink wine. They descended and began to judge among the people with truth; when night fell they would pronounce the Name and return to the exalted assembly, and at dawn they would descend again, and each night, having pronounced the Name that Allāh had taught them, they would ascend once more to heaven — until a woman came before them disputing with her husband, and the two stood before them. When one of the angels looked upon her, she seized hold of his heart; he turned to his companion and said, "Has the like of what has befallen me befallen you?" His companion said, "Yes — what do you propose?" He said, "Let us simply try to win her." His companion said, "Do you not know that Allāh is severe in punishment?" He said, "He is Forgiving, Merciful." So the two of them solicited the woman, but she refused them unless they would rule in her favour against her husband; they did so, ruling that she was in the right. She then said she would not be content unless they drank wine with her; each, intending only that single transgression, said to himself, "Allāh is severe in punishment," and the other answered, "He is Forgiving, Merciful," and they drank. She then said this alone did not satisfy her — they must kill him — so they solicited her again, and killed him. Then she said they must prostrate to an idol, and they did so. Thus they fell, one after another, into everything they had been forbidden. When night came she said they must teach her the Greatest Name by which they ascended to heaven; they taught it to her, she pronounced it, and flew up to heaven, and was transformed — she is, so it is said, the star Venus to this day. As for the two angels, when night fell they found themselves unable to fly up to heaven, so they went to Idrīs, the prophet of that age, and Allāh revealed to him that they must choose between the punishment of this world and the punishment of the next; they chose the punishment of this world, for its being transient, and were cast into a well, where they remain being punished to this day. This is the story of Hārūt and Mārūt, and it is a fabrication with no foundation.

Consider, too, envy (ḥasad)! Envy is among the grave sins. The Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., said, "Envy devours good deeds as fire devours firewood," for the envier detests the favour of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, upon another, and thereby imputes injustice to the Creator, sets himself against the friend of Allāh, and strives to thwart the power of Allāh and to obstruct His servants from obeying Him. The envious man is never given dominion — for man is by nature disinclined to submit to being subordinate; no one loves to be a follower of his equal save for some compelling reason, and when that reason lapses, the following lapses with it. Even our Creator has conferred upon us benefits and favours that require of us that we be obedient to Him, and whoever desires the removal of these favours has thereby desired the removal of the very ground of his obedience to Him, Blessed and Exalted is He. The envious man harms none but himself; you will find no wrongdoer more akin to the one he wrongs than the envier — whatever harm he contrives against the one he envies rebounds upon himself, so that his rage only increases and returns upon him. He is like a man who takes up a stone meaning to hurl it at another's hand to break it, but the throw rebounds and breaks his own hand; he hurls a second stone and it strikes his other hand; a third, and it puts out his own eye; a fourth, and it puts out his other eye. Such is the way of the envier.

There was once a king who was regularly visited by a learned man held in high regard, who used to counsel him, whenever he sat with him, "Do good to whoever does good to you, and let the one who does evil be requited by his own evil-doing." A man who lived nearby envied him his standing with the king, and went to the king and said, "So-and-so, whom you draw near to you, speaks ill of you — he says you have foul breath." The king said, "How am I to know he says such a thing?" The man replied, "When he next comes near you, watch him closely, and you will see that he says you have foul breath." He then went and invited the learned man to his house and fed him leeks, onions, and garlic — foods with a strong odour — and afterward told him, "Go; the king summons you." When he came and greeted the king with his usual counsel, the king said, "Come closer," meaning to confide in him; the man, still smelling of the meal, covered his mouth with his garment, and this confirmed for the king what had been said of him. The king, enraged, wrote — for he never wrote save to grant a reward — a letter with his own hand to one of his officials: "When the bearer of this letter reaches you, slaughter him, flay him, stuff his skin with straw, and send it back to me at once." He sealed it and gave it to the learned man. The envious man then came to him and pleaded until the learned man gave him the letter. He took it to the official and handed it over; the official read it and said, "The king has commanded me to slaughter you and stuff your skin with straw and send it to him." The man said, "No — I am not the one to whom this letter refers; go back to the king, I am not the bearer he intended." The official replied, "I do not refer matters back to the king — I carry out his command without question," and so he slaughtered him, flayed him, and sent his skin, stuffed with straw, to the king. When the king saw it and realised it was the envious man, he sent for the learned man. The learned man came in and said, as always, "Do good to whoever does good to you, and let the one who does evil be requited by his own evil-doing." The king said, "Tell me your story — did you not say I have foul breath?" He answered, "God forbid — how should I speak of the king in a way I have no knowledge of?" The king said, "Then why, when I drew you near, did you cover your mouth?" He said, "Because so-and-so had fed me food with a strong odour, and I disliked that the king should catch any unpleasant scent from me." The king said, "Do good to whoever does good to you, and let the one who does evil be requited by his own evil-doing."

"Or do you wish to question your Messenger as Moses was questioned before?" — meaning, do you truly wish to question the Messenger of Allāh, ṣ.a.w.s., with such demands? The occasion of revelation of this verse is that the people of Mecca gathered and came to the Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., saying to him, "If you wish us to believe in you, then widen the lands of Mecca for us; remove these mountains so that we may find land to cultivate; turn al-Ṣafā into gold for us; raise up for us Quṣayy ibn Kilāb, our forefather, so that we may take counsel with him about believing in you; and bring us the angels, that they may bear witness that you are truly a prophet." "Whoever exchanges faith for disbelief has certainly strayed from the sound way" — this refers to one who turns away from the manifest signs already given him and demands others in their place; whoever looks upon the clear signs and yet insists on demanding still others is thereby forsaken. He who has truly gone astray finds that seeking further signs avails nothing; whoever Allāh intends for faith sees the sign before even asking for it, while whoever, having witnessed these signs again and again, continues to demand yet more, stands condemned only to wretchedness and abandonment.

"Many of the People of the Book wish they could turn you back to disbelief after your having believed, out of envy from themselves." ʿAmmār and Bilāl, returning from the Prophet's campaigns, ṣ.a.w.s., used to pass by a church and stop to listen to the sermons, in which they found certain traditions that reinforced their faith in the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., and in the Qurʾān. When the Jews saw the two of them listening, they hoped to draw them back into apostasy, and began inviting them to their religion and pressing them toward it. ʿAmmār said to them, "How, in your reckoning, is a covenant broken?" They said, "As by killing." He said, "We have covenanted with Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s., to follow him for the rest of our lives, and we will never break that covenant" — and it was on this occasion that this verse was revealed: "Many of the People of the Book wish they could turn you back to disbelief after your having believed, out of envy from themselves" — their own base selves drove them to this envy — "even after the truth has become clear to them" — in the Torah, concerning the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s. — "So pardon and overlook" them, that is, leave them be — "until Allāh brings His command" concerning them, meaning the command to fight — "indeed, Allāh is over all things competent" — this being abrogated by the verse of the sword. "And establish prayer and give zakāt" — whenever He enjoins prayer, He mentions zakāt alongside it, prayer being the foremost of bodily devotions and zakāt the foremost of devotions involving wealth. "And whatever good you put forward for yourselves" — obedience, such as ties of kinship and almsgiving — "you will find it with Allāh" — you will find its reward with Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He — "Indeed, Allāh is Seeing of what you do," and will requite you for it accordingly.

ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb once rode out, in his time, to al-Baqīʿ, and greeted the people of the graveyard, saying, "Word of what has happened after you: your wealth has been divided, your houses have been settled by others, your wives have remarried — such is the news of what has come after you." He heard a voice reply: "But what we sent ahead we have found, and what we left behind we have lost." This bears out the meaning of His saying, "And whatever good you put forward for yourselves, you will find it with Allāh."

The Jews say, "None shall enter Paradise except one who is a Jew" — only he who is a Jew, in their reckoning, enters Paradise. "Or a Christian" — the Christians of Najrān likewise say that none enters Paradise but a Christian. This was their claim when they disputed before the Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s.: the Jews swore none would enter Paradise but the Jews, and the Christians swore none but the Christians. Allāh, Exalted is He, said: "That" claim "is but their own wishful thinking" — their vain desires. "Say: Produce your proof, if you should be truthful" — that others besides yourselves may enter Paradise. "Rather, whoever submits his face to Allāh" — surrenders to His command; the face is singled out for mention because it is the noblest of the members, so that whatever is true of it applies still more to the rest — "and is virtuous" — a true worshipper of the One God — "will have his reward with his Lord" — the reward of his deeds being Paradise — "and no fear will there be concerning them, nor will they grieve." Islām, īmān, and iḥsān together constitute the religion of the Chosen One, ṣ.a.w.s. A man once came to the assembly of the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s. — bearing no mark of travel, his garments of dazzling white — and sat before him until his knees touched the knees of the Chosen One, ṣ.a.w.s., and said, "Tell me of islām." The Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., said, "It is to bear witness that there is no god but Allāh and that I am the Messenger of Allāh, to perform the prayer, to give zakāt, to fast Ramaḍān, and to make the pilgrimage to the House if you are able to find a way there." He said, "You have spoken truly." Then he said, "Tell me of īmān." He said, "It is that you believe in Allāh, His angels, His books, and His messengers, and that you believe in the divine decree, its good and its evil." He said, "You have spoken truly." We marvelled at him, that he should question the Prophet and then confirm what he said. Then he said, "Tell me of iḥsān." He said, "It is that you worship Allāh as though you see Him; for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you." He said, "You have spoken truly." Then he said, "When is the Hour?" He said, "The one questioned about it knows no more than the questioner; but I will tell you of its signs: when the slave-girl gives birth to her mistress" — meaning that concubinage will become so widespread that a slave-girl bears her own master's child, so that her child becomes, in effect, her owner — "and when you see the barefoot, the naked, the destitute herdsmen of sheep vying with one another in building." Then the man departed, and the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., said, "Go after him," but they sought him and found no trace of him. He said to them, "Do you know who the questioner was?" They said, "No." He said, "That was Gabriel, come to teach you your religion." Whoever enters into Islām by saying, "There is no god but Allāh, Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allāh," has thereby earned his reward with his Lord, and no fear shall be upon him, nor shall he grieve.

Allāh revealed to the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., that on this same day Diḥya al-Kalbī would come to him as a Muslim; and Diḥya had, in the Age of Ignorance, committed deeds the Companions found repugnant. When the time came for him to enter, the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., being seated among a gathering of his Companions, and Diḥya appearing, the Chosen One, ṣ.a.w.s., disliked that they should confront him with anything unbecoming, lest it turn him away from Islām, and he did not wish to command them concerning it; so when Diḥya entered, the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., took up his own cloak, spread it out, and said to Diḥya, "Sit here." Diḥya came, folded the cloak, kissed it, and pressed it to his heart, saying, "What a noble character is this!" He sat and said, "Teach me Islām." The Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., said to him, "Say: I bear witness that there is no god but Allāh, and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allāh." He pronounced the two testimonies and began to weep bitterly. The Chosen One, ṣ.a.w.s., said to him, "What makes you weep, now that you were a disbeliever and Allāh has guided you to Islām?" He said, "I did something in the Age of Ignorance that I do not think Allāh will forgive me." The Chosen One pressed him, and he answered, "I was among the Arabs who despised having daughters; Allāh granted me seventy daughters, and I killed every one of them. I fear that Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, will not forgive me this sin." The Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., fell silent, and Gabriel descended and said to him, "Tell him that Allāh has forgiven him sixty years of sin and sixty years of disbelief — how should He not forgive him the killing of his daughters, when this single utterance effaces all that came before it?" Whoever has been a disbeliever for hundreds or thousands of years, if he utters this word, emerges from his sins as on the day his mother bore him. The Companions asked, "Is this for Diḥya alone, or for us as well?" The Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., said, "Rather, it is for you all," and they rejoiced at this. The Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., said, "O Lord, You forgave Diḥya al-Kalbī sixty years of disbelief and sixty years of sin with a single word — how, then, should You not forgive my community, who repeat it night and day, believing in it?"

"The Jews say, 'The Christians stand on nothing,'" worthy of regard, and they disbelieve in Jesus — "and the Christians say, 'The Jews stand on nothing,'" worthy of regard, and they disbelieve in Moses — "though they both recite the Scripture" — the revealed Books; the Jews refuse to affirm Jesus even though they read the Torah, in which his coming is affirmed, and the Christians do likewise. "Thus," just as these have said, "do those who do not know" — the polytheists of the Arabs and others — "say the like of what they say" — that every adherent of a different religion stands on nothing. "Allāh will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection concerning that in which they used to differ" — concerning matters of religion.

"And who is more unjust than one who bars the mosques of Allāh, that His name be mentioned therein" — this was revealed either when the pagans barred the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., from the House in the year of al-Ḥudaybiya, or concerning the Romans who destroyed Jerusalem, or concerning the polytheists, or concerning all of them together — "and strives toward their destruction?" — no one is more unjust than one who bars the mosques of Allāh from having His name mentioned in them, through prayer and glorification, and strives for their ruin, whether by demolition or by disuse; and this applies to anyone who bars a mosque from anyone devoted to worship or the remembrance of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He. "It is not for them to enter them except in fear" — meaning they are to be kept in fear of it, through struggle waged against them — "for them in this world is disgrace" — through killing, captivity, and tribute — "and for them in the Hereafter is a great punishment" — the Fire.

"To Allāh belong the east and the west" — the occasion of revelation of this verse is that the Jews impugned the abrogation of the qibla. The Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., possessed no single qibla, for the true qibla is every direction — he prayed toward every direction, "for wherever you turn, there is the Face of Allāh" — and it was only afterward that he was commanded to pray toward Jerusalem, which was then abrogated, and he was commanded to pray toward the Kaʿba; and the Jews impugned this change of the qibla, whereupon this verse was revealed: "To Allāh belong the east and the west," meaning: all directions belong to Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, alone. "So wherever you turn" your faces in prayer, truly — "there is the Face (wajh) of Allāh" — His qibla, the one He has approved — "indeed, Allāh is All-Encompassing" — His bounty encompasses all things — "All-Knowing." This is because the Christians' qibla is the east alone: "And mention in the Book Maryam, when she withdrew from her family to an eastern place" — because the birthplace of Jesus was in an eastern place, they sanctified the east and said no prayer is valid save toward it; while the Jews' qibla is the west, "and you were not on the western side" — because Moses' colloquy with Allāh took place on the western side, and so they chose the west as their qibla. The Muslims, however, submitted to the command of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, without inclining toward anything of their own desire, and it so happened that their qibla coincided with being the qibla of Ibrāhīm — they being upon the religion of Ibrāhīm — and with the birthplace of Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s. What the other two communities had each contrived for themselves came, for the Muslims, unsought; their sole aim was compliance with the command of Allāh alone.

"So wherever you turn, there is the Face of Allāh" — His qibla; this is the interpretation of the interpreters — or, in a deeper sense, "wherever you turn, there is the Face of Allāh" means: His direction, His very Essence — for the Essence has no direction and no place, so that wherever the servant turns he faces It. What perfect gnosis therefore requires of the servant is that he might pray toward whatever direction he wishes; but our praying toward a single direction is a matter of discipline and of compliance with the command of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, and a training of the soul so that it may become accustomed to due order. Concentrating one's direction in worship: when the servant, every time he sets out to worship, to supplicate, or to remember Allāh, faces a single direction, this is best, for the outward concentrating of the servant's direction has its effect on the inward, until Allāh grants him the concentrating of his direction inwardly as well. The true qibla is the exalted Essence itself; what is required of the servant is that his heart be turned always and eternally toward this true qibla. Repeatedly facing the outward qibla may in this way discipline the inward being of the servant until he comes to face the true qibla; were it not for this, it would be irksome for the spiritually realised knower (al-ʿārif) to keep praying toward one fixed direction, for to him all directions are truly one.

"And they say, 'Allāh has taken a son'" — the Jews, the Christians, and whoever claims the angels are the daughters of Allāh. He said, Exalted is He: "Glory be to Him" — He is exalted far above this — "rather, to Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth," as sovereignty, creation, and possession, and ownership is incompatible with begetting — a man, indeed, cannot in law own his own child; whoever, in the Sharīʿa, comes to own his child, that child is thereby set free. "All are devoutly obedient to Him" — submissive, each doing as is required of it. "Originator of the heavens and the earth" — their Bringer-into-being without any prior model — "and when He decrees" — wills — "a matter, He only says to it, 'Be,' and it is" — this being an expression of the manifestation of divine power.

"And those who do not know say" — the disbelievers of Mecca, to the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s. — "Why does Allāh not speak to us," that you are indeed His messenger, "or a sign come to us" — some sign of their own devising. "Thus" did those before them say; "those before them said the like of their words" — the same obstinacy and demand for signs — "their hearts resemble one another" — in disbelief and stubbornness. In this there is consolation for the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s.: "We have certainly made clear the signs for a people who are certain" — who know them to be signs and so believe, so that demanding still another sign alongside them is mere obstinacy.

"Indeed, We have sent you," O Muḥammad, "with the truth," with guidance, "as a bringer of good tidings" — to whoever answers it, of Paradise — "and a warner" — to whoever does not answer it, of the Fire — "and you will not be asked about the companions of the Hellfire" — the disbelievers who did not believe; it is not incumbent upon you to account for why they did not believe — your task is only to convey the message. "And never will the Jews or the Christians approve of you until you follow their religion" — neither the Jews nor the Christians will ever be pleased with the Chosen One, nor with the Muslims, unless they follow them in their religion.

"Say, 'Indeed, the guidance of Allāh is the only guidance,'" which is Islām, and whatever else there may be is misguidance — "and if you were to follow their inclinations after the knowledge which has come to you, you would have, against Allāh, no protector and no helper." "Those to whom We have given the Scripture, who recite it as it should be recited" — as it was revealed — "those are the ones who believe in it" — believe truly in the Qurʾān — "and whoever disbelieves in it" — in the revealed Scripture, by distorting it — "it is those who are the losers" — destined for the eternal Fire. "O Children of Israel, remember My favour which I bestowed upon you and that I preferred you over the worlds" — the like of this has already been mentioned: the Truth called upon the Children of Israel and reminded them of the favours He had bestowed upon their forebears, and of the excellence He had granted their forebears, so that they might remember and give thanks, and thereby believe and stand firm. The difference between the community of Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s. — "the best community brought forth for mankind" — and the Children of Israel, is this: the Children of Israel, when Allāh reminded them of His favour to them, grew arrogant and angry, and so were destroyed on account of the very excellence they had witnessed; whereas the community of Muḥammad — "Say: In the bounty of Allāh and in His mercy — in that let them rejoice" — when we remember the favour of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, our hope in Him only grows the stronger; whereas they, whenever they were reminded of their own excellence, grew arrogant, and what befell Iblīs befell them. "And fear a Day when no soul will suffice for another soul at all" — will avail it nothing — "nor will any compensation be accepted from it, nor will intercession benefit it, nor will they be helped" — that is, the Day of Resurrection; they were warned of that Day, for when a man in this world faces some calamity, another may sometimes come to his aid and rescue him from it; and if he finds no such helper, he may perhaps offer wealth and thereby escape the calamity; and if he finds neither of these, perhaps men of standing will intervene and intercede for him, and the intercession will avail him; and if he finds none of this, nothing remains for him but to fight, drawing on the support of kinsmen and loved ones who will stand by him in the struggle. Allāh has negated all of these avenues on the Day of Resurrection: "No soul will suffice for another soul at all, nor will any compensation be accepted from it, nor will intercession benefit it, nor will they be helped" — they will find no one to intercede for them until their intercession is accepted; they will have no intercessor, unlike the Muslims.

"And mention when Ibrāhīm was tried" — tested — "by his Lord with words" — commands and prohibitions with which He charged him. It is said these were the rites of the pilgrimage; it is also said they were: rinsing the mouth, sniffing water into the nostrils, using the tooth-stick, trimming the moustache, parting the hair, paring the nails, plucking the armpit hair, shaving the pubic hair, ritual cleansing after relieving oneself, and circumcision — he being the first to be circumcised, at the age of eighty, using an adze. It is also said these were thirty qualities: ten in Sūrat Barāʾa [al-Tawba], "those who turn in repentance, who worship..." [Q. 9:112]; ten in Sūrat al-Muʾminūn, "Certainly will the believers have succeeded..." [Q. 23:1] through "those are the inheritors" [Q. 23:10]; and ten in Sūrat al-Aḥzāb, "Indeed, the Muslim men and Muslim women..." [Q. 33:35] — these thirty qualities being what Ibrāhīm's Lord tried him with — "and he fulfilled them" — discharged them completely. "He said" — Exalted is He, to him — "Indeed, I will make you a leader for the people" — an exemplar in religion, every adherent of every religion claiming to follow the religion of Ibrāhīm. "He said, 'And of my descendants?'" — meaning, make my children also leaders in religion. "He said, 'My covenant does not include the wrongdoers'" — the disbelievers. Ibrāhīm had not known that any of his descendants would be disbelievers, until this answer came.

"And mention when We made the House" — the Kaʿba — "a place of return for the people" — a resort to which they return, that is, repair to it from every side — "and a place of security" — a refuge for them from wrongdoing and the depredations that occur elsewhere: a man might meet, within it, the very slayer of his father, and yet would not molest him there. "And take, [O believers], from the Station of Ibrāhīm a place of prayer" — the Station being the stone on which Ibrāhīm stood while building the House — meaning a place of prayer, that you should pray the two rakʿas of ṭawāf behind the Station. "And We charged Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl" — commanded them both — "that they purify My House" — of idols — "for those who circle it or seclude themselves therein for devotion" — those in retreat there — "or bow or prostrate" — meaning those who pray. "And mention when Ibrāhīm said, 'My Lord, make this'" place "'a secure city'" — for it was then, when he prayed, a wilderness, and the place was barren, hence his saying "a city." In Sūrat Ibrāhīm he said, "My Lord, make this city secure" [Q. 14:35] — the prayer in Sūrat Ibrāhīm having come after Allāh had already answered his supplication, when Mecca had become an inhabited town, and He had made it a sanctuary in which no human blood is shed, no one is wronged, no game is hunted, and no plant is cut. "And provide its people with fruits" — which He indeed did, by transporting produce to it from al-Ṭāʾif and from Syria, though it was otherwise a barren place with neither cultivation nor water. "...such of them as believe in Allāh and the Last Day" — he had sought the leadership in religion for his descendants and was refused it, for the unjust are barred from that leadership, and so, in seeking sustenance, he said, "such of them as believe." He said, "And whoever disbelieves, I will grant him enjoyment for a little, then I will force him to the punishment of the Fire, and wretched is the destination."

The House of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He — which He made a place of return for the people and a place of security — is the Kaʿba which stands at Mecca, or at Bakka, Bakka being either another name for Mecca, or simply the site upon which the Kaʿba stands. The Kaʿba has been built ten times: the first building was by the angels; the second by Adam; then it was built by Shīth, son of Adam, ancestor of the Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s.; then Ibrāhīm built it a fourth time, after the Flood, and it is his building that is referred to in this verse. After Ibrāhīm it was built by the ʿAmāliqa, then by Jurhum, then by Quṣayy ibn Kilāb, then by Quraysh, then by ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr, and then by al-Ḥajjāj — al-Ḥajjāj's building being the one still standing today. As for the angels, they had once slandered Adam, and afterward sought to repent to Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He; they possess the Frequented House (al-bayt al-maʿmūr), the celestial Kaʿba, around which they circle continually, and Allāh commanded them to build for Adam a house like it; so they built the Kaʿba directly beneath the Frequented House, such that were the Frequented House to fall to earth, it would fall precisely upon the Kaʿba. Adam used to make pilgrimage to the House — he made the pilgrimage forty times on foot from the land of India to Mecca. It was asked of the Messenger, "What did he ride?" He said, "What beast could bear Adam? No beast could bear Adam, not even the elephant" — he walked on his own feet from the land of India to Mecca, making the pilgrimage forty times. Then, when the Flood of Noah came, the angels raised the Kaʿba up to heaven, and took the Black Stone and deposited it, as a trust, in the mountain of Abū Qubays, beside the Kaʿba.

As time passed and the age of Ibrāhīm arrived, Ibrāhīm was then in the lowlands of Iraq, in the districts of Kūfa, and he migrated toward Palestine, part of Syria. Along the way of his migration there stood a tyrant's toll-post: no man passed with a beautiful wife but that she was seized from him. With Ibrāhīm was his wife Sāra, the most beautiful woman on earth — indeed, the beauty of Joseph was owing only to his resemblance to Sāra. As Ibrāhīm drew near the toll-post — and it was the custom there that whoever brought a beautiful woman and said she was his wife, she would be seized from him, whereas if he said she was his sister, she would be left with him — Ibrāhīm said to Sāra: "There is no Muslim on the face of this earth save you and I; you are, then, my sister" — and indeed she was, moreover, his cousin. "Should they ask me, I shall say you are my sister" — this being one of the equivocations for which he will apologise on the Day of Resurrection. Ibrāhīm, being intensely jealous, fashioned a chest, placed the woman inside it, and locked it. When he came to the toll-collector, the man asked him what was in the chest. He said, "Name whatever price you like, and I will pay it." He said, "If you say the chest is full of gold, I will take a quarter of it as gold" — this being the toll then in force, a quarter of the goods. The man said, "No — not until I open the chest," and upon opening it he found the most beautiful of all women within it. He said, "What is she to you?" Ibrāhīm said, "She is my sister." So the man took her and brought her before the king, in violation of the toll-custom, so overpowering was the woman's beauty; but Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, lifted the veils from before Ibrāhīm, so that Sāra never vanished from his sight from the moment she was brought into the palace and into its chambers, and Ibrāhīm could see her in all her circumstances; the All-Glorious preserved her from any suspicion touching her. When she was brought before the king and he reached out his hand toward her, he at once fell down insensible; he resolved to repent and his senses returned to him, but when he reached toward her again he fell down more violently than before. When he recovered, he said to her, "Are you a sorceress?" She said, "No — I am the wife of the Friend (Khalīl) of Allāh, and the All-Glorious protects me." He said, "Give her some adornment," so they pierced her ear and placed something in it, which became so fine a thing that women have done the like of it to this day. He gave her a slave-girl, Hājar, as a gift, and returned her to her husband. She said to Ibrāhīm, "Rejoice — Allāh has restrained the enemy, and has granted me a maidservant to serve me. O Ibrāhīm, I am barren; I have borne you no child, and I present this slave-girl to you, that you may take her as a concubine, so that Allāh may grant you offspring through her." He took her as a concubine, and she bore Ismāʿīl. Jealousy, however, can also occasion conception — for when the slave-girl conceived, Sāra also conceived, and bore Isḥāq.

Ismāʿīl, being older than Isḥāq, would strike Isḥāq when the two went out to play, and Isḥāq would come weeping to his mother; Sāra grew angry at this and said, "This was a slave-girl I once owned, and now her son strikes my son every day; I can bear it no longer — take this slave-girl away; I will not have her near me ever again." Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, mounted his beast, took up Hājar and Ismāʿīl behind him, and journeyed with them from Syria to the land of the Kaʿba, which was then a barren waste; and when he arrived he left them there with a skin of water and a sack of dates, and turned to go. The woman began calling after him, "O Ibrāhīm, O Ibrāhīm!" while Ibrāhīm continued walking on, until at last she said, "Has Allāh commanded you to do this, O Ibrāhīm?" He said, "Yes." She said, "Then go wherever you wish; if it is Allāh's command, He will not let us be lost." After some days had passed, the water and provisions they had were exhausted, and Ismāʿīl grew so thirsty he nearly died; Hājar went out until she reached the hill of al-Ṣafā and climbed it, looking to see whether she might spy water or any person — she saw nothing — so she went to al-Marwa, then returned to al-Ṣafā, and went again to al-Marwa, seven times in all — this being the origin of the saʿy between the two hills — and then returned to her child, expecting only death. Then Gabriel came and struck the ground with his foot, and the spring of Zamzam burst forth, and the water flowed until it became like a sea; Gabriel restrained the water, saying, "Zam, zam," meaning "be still," until it settled into a well. The Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., said, "May Allāh have mercy on the mother of Ismāʿīl — had she left Zamzam alone, it would have remained a flowing spring" — for the depth of Zamzam is known to none but Allāh; within it lies a sea, its waters running beneath Mecca entirely; and so the woman drank, and the child drank.

There were, meanwhile, some hunters who dwelt in that wasteland and had seen no trace of water there; they noticed a bird circling above where the woman was, and said, "Glory be to Allāh — such birds are never seen except where there is water; but where could water be found in this wasteland?" So they came and found the well of Zamzam, with Hājar and Ismāʿīl there, and said to her, "May we drink of your water?" She said, "Yes, but you have no right to it." So they drank, and they were the tribe of Jurhum, Arabs; afterward they asked to settle near her, and did so, and Ismāʿīl grew up among them, an Arab.

After some time had passed, Ibrāhīm asked Sāra's leave to visit Hājar and his son Ismāʿīl, and she consented, on condition that he not dismount from his camel. Ibrāhīm came and found Ismāʿīl away, and found a woman in the house; he said, "Peace be upon you — where is your husband?" She said, "I do not know where he has gone." He said, "Are you well provided for?" She said, "No, we are not well off — my husband has gone hunting; if he finds something we shall eat, and if not, we shall be in a hard state — and how much you do ask!" He said, "When your husband comes, give him my greeting, and tell him to change the threshold of his door." When her husband returned she related this to him, saying, "A man came here and asked me many questions, and told me to tell you to change the threshold of your door." He said, "That was my father Ibrāhīm; he has commanded me to divorce you," and he said, "You are divorced." After some further time, Sāra again gave Ibrāhīm leave to visit, on the same condition that he not dismount from his camel; Ismāʿīl by then had had Hājar pass away, and had married again. He came again and found Ismāʿīl out hunting; when the second wife saw him she said, "Is this my father-in-law? How handsome a man — how much he resembles my husband! Nothing distinguishes between them but the grey hair. Do come down!" He said, "No, I will not dismount — but how are you all?" She said, "We are well; we lack for nothing, and my husband will be here presently — do wait; let me wash your feet." Ibrāhīm said, "I will not dismount," but she brought a stone from the Station and placed it beneath Ibrāhīm's foot and washed his right foot, and then washed the other — which is why the mark of Ibrāhīm's foot remains upon that stone to this day. When Ismāʿīl came he said, "I smell the scent of Ibrāhīm in this house." She said, "A man came here, indistinguishable from you save for the grey hair; he greeted you and said you should fix firmly the threshold of your door." He said, "That was Ibrāhīm; he has commanded me to keep you and not to divorce you."

Then Ibrāhīm returned, by the command of Allāh, Blessed and Exalted is He, and said, "O Ismāʿīl, Allāh has commanded me to build for Him a House here, and has commanded you to help me." He said, "Yes, I will help you," and the two of them set about building the House. Ibrāhīm spoke Hebrew and Ismāʿīl spoke Arabic, yet each understood the other, though neither spoke the other's tongue: when Ismāʿīl spoke, Ibrāhīm listened and did not speak, and when Ibrāhīm spoke, Ismāʿīl listened and did not speak; and so the two of them built the Kaʿba — this same Kaʿba that stands today at Mecca, possessed of many virtues, which will be mentioned when we come to Sūrat Āl ʿImrān: "In it are clear signs, such as the standing place of Ibrāhīm; and whoever enters it shall be safe" [Q. 3:97] — whoever makes the pilgrimage without lewd speech or transgression returns from his sins as on the day his mother bore him. As they built, whenever Ibrāhīm laid a stone, the two of them would say, "Our Lord, accept this from us, indeed You are the Hearing, the Knowing," until the building rose so high that the old man could no longer lift the stones up to it, whereupon he stood upon the stone of the Station and Ismāʿīl handed him the stones from there, and the two of them continued saying, "Our Lord, accept this from us, indeed You are the Hearing, the Knowing."
