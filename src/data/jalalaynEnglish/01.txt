[1:1]
{In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate}
[1:2]
{Praise belongs to God} — a declarative sentence by whose content laudation of God is intended, on the understanding that He, exalted be He, owns every praise that creatures render, or merits that they praise Him. *Allāh* is a proper name for the One rightly worshipped. {Lord of the worlds} that is, Owner of the whole of creation — humankind, jinn, angels, beasts and others besides — each of which is called a "world" (*ʿālam*); one says "the world of humankind", "the world of the jinn", and so forth. In the plural formed with *yāʾ* and *nūn* [*ʿālamīn*], those endowed with intellect predominate over the rest. The word derives from *ʿalāma* (mark), since [creation] is a mark pointing to the One who brought it into being.
[1:3]
{the Merciful, the Compassionate} that is, Possessor of mercy, mercy being the willing of good for those to whom it is due.
[1:4]
{Master of the Day of Judgement} that is, of requital, which is the Day of Resurrection. It is singled out for mention because on that Day no sovereignty is outwardly anyone's save God's, exalted be He, on the evidence of {To whom belongs sovereignty this Day? To God} [Q. 40:16]. Whoever recites *mālik* [Owner] takes the sense to be: Owner of all command when the Resurrection comes; or else the description belongs to Him perpetually, as with *ghāfir al-dhanb* (Forgiver of sin), and on that reading it stands soundly as an attribute qualifying a definite noun.
[1:5]
{You alone do we worship, and of You alone do we seek aid} that is, we reserve worship for You — professing [Your] oneness (*tawḥīd*) and the rest — and we ask for help in worship and in all else.
[1:6]
{Guide us along the straight path} that is, direct us to it. Substituted for it is:
[1:7]
{the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour} — favour by way of guidance. And the substitute for *alladhīna*, taken with the clause that completes it, is: {not of those who incur anger} — these being the Jews — {nor of} and not of {those who go astray} — these being the Christians. The purpose of the substitution is to establish that the rightly guided are neither Jews nor Christians. God knows best where correctness lies; to Him is the place of return and the journey's end.
