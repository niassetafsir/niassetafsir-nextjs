# ق/ف in the prose — decision worksheet

Generated by `scripts/build-prose-ocr-worksheet.js`. **Nothing here has been applied.**

Every ق/ف repair *inside* a Qurʾānic quotation has already been made and proved against the
muṣḥaf. What follows is the remainder: suspect tokens in Niasse's own prose, where the Qurʾān
has no standing and the decision is lexical.

A token appears here only if the Qurʾān does not spell it that way **and** one ق/ف swap turns
it into a word the Qurʾān does spell. That gate produces the real errors and the false alarms
alike — right about فال → قال, wrong about لقي → لفي — and frequency cannot separate them,
because a damaged form is common precisely *because* the damage is systematic: لفد occurs 45
times against لقد's 63, and the ratio says nothing.

## What sorts them

2,383 corrections have already been made inside quotations, each confirmed by the āya being
quoted, across 1,173 distinct word pairs. Every one is a demonstrated fact about this OCR: it turns
*this* word into *that* word. Where a prose token matches a pair proved that way, the misreading
is attested in the source itself, repeatedly, under Qurʾānic control. Where it does not, nothing
here vouches for it.

| **Attested** | 83 | 849 | this exact pair proved ≥3× inside quotations |
| **Thinly attested** | 142 | 352 | proved once or twice — suggestive, not settled |
| **Unproved** | 605 | 1030 | never confirmed anywhere; **the lexicon cases** |

Triage, not a verdict. Even a well-attested pair can be wrong in a particular clause — a word
Niasse chose that happens to collide with a form the OCR also produces — so contexts are printed
rather than a batch offered.

And the caution no count can encode: **attested in Lane is not warrant to emend.** The proposed
form has to be attested *in the required morphology*, to fit the clause syntactically and
semantically, and the confusion has to be plausible for this source — which for a Maghribī
printing read by a Mashriqī-trained OCR, it generally is.
