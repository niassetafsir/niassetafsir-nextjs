## Sūrat al-Baqara — Lesson Six

*I seek refuge in God from the accursed Satan. In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate. O God, bless our master Muḥammad, the opener of what was closed and the seal of what preceded, the helper of truth by truth, the guide to Your straight path, and [bless] his family as befits his tremendous rank and measure, and may God be pleased with the Companions of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him. There is no power and no strength save in God, the Exalted, the Tremendous.*

*O aspiration of the Shaykh, attend for us at this gathering,*
*incline toward us with a glance,*
*that brings us to triumph.*

**[169]** "**And remember God** by [saying] *Allāhu akbar* at the throwing of the pebbles, **in the numbered days**" — the three days of *tashrīq*. "**Whoever hastens [his departure] in two days**" — on the second of the *tashrīq* days, after throwing his pebbles — "**there is no sin upon him**" for hastening, "**and whoever delays**" [his departure] until he spends the third night and throws his pebbles then, "**there is no sin upon him either**" — meaning they are given the choice, and sin is negated either way, "**for whoever is God-fearing**" — who feared God in his ḥajj, for he is the true pilgrim. "**And fear God, and know that unto Him you shall be gathered**" — in the hereafter, where He will requite you for your deeds.

The Exalted mentions in these verses the rites of ḥajj, and commands His servants to complete the ḥajj and the ʿumra for God's sake alone, without ostentation or reputation-seeking — for ḥajj is the most ostentation-prone of acts of worship: you will not find a pilgrim to whom some piece of news is presented but that he mentions some anecdote about the Ḥijāz, boasting of his pilgrimage. Hence He said, "**Complete the ḥajj and the ʿumra for God**," and commanded excellence (*iḥsān*) — excellence of the servant in his words and his deeds, excellence in his intention, and excellence in his opinion, in that he should think well of God, Blessed and Exalted, and of His servants. The Lord says on the tongue of His Prophet: "I am as My servant thinks of Me," and the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, says: "Let none of you die except while thinking well of God." And He commanded His servants, once they complete their ḥajj, to multiply the remembrance of God as they remember their fathers, or even more intensely.

Ḥajj outwardly is the intention toward the House and the performance of its rites; the true ḥajj is the return to God, Blessed and Exalted. So whoever completes that ḥajj until he arrives at the [divine] Presence should multiply the remembrance of God, concerned with nothing but God, until he tastes the remembrance of God, Blessed and Exalted. Hence He addressed the gnostics (*al-ʿārifīn*): "**When you have completed your rites**" — by the true ḥajj — "**then remember God as you remember your fathers**." A child in his infancy remembers his father in every need that arises for him, and in his manhood he takes pride in mentioning his father. Just as the servant remembers his father in both states — pride and need — the gnostic of God remembers his Lord in pride and in need, indeed more intensely than that: for the child may become independent of his father through some other guardian, whereas the gnostic finds no independence from God, Blessed and Exalted, and sees no one besides Him. Hence He urged the multiplication of God's remembrance after arrival at God's Presence, Blessed and Exalted.

People fall into [four groups] according to their aim: some aim only at this world; some want this world and the next; some want this world and sell their hereafter for it; and some sell this world for their hereafter — four categories, all of which God mentions in this verse. Among them is "**he who says, 'Our Lord, give us in this world,'**" seeking this world only, "**and he has no share in the hereafter**." And among them is "**he who says, 'Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire**'" — this one seeks the good of this world and the good of the hereafter. The third type will come in the verse "**Among the people is he whose speech about the life of this world impresses you, and he calls God to witness what is in his heart, while he is the fiercest of adversaries**" — this one sold his hereafter for his worldly life. The fourth is the most complete of them: he sold himself and bought the hereafter — "**And remember God in the numbered days**."

It is said "numbered days" (*ayyām maʿdūdāt*), the plural of *maʿdūda* (feminine), and one does not say "a numbered day" [in the masculine] — the masculine is not qualified with the feminine [form of the adjective]; but since it is permissible to say "numbered days" [in the sense encountered before] as in "**the Fire shall not touch us except for a number of days**" (*ayyāman maʿdūda*) — describing a [masculine] plural with a feminine [singular] adjective is permitted — hence "*maʿdūdāt*" here is the plural of *maʿdūda*, a feminine-form plural adjective. Mālik and al-Shāfiʿī differed over which days are "the numbered days": Mālik says it is the Day of Sacrifice and the days following it; al-Shāfiʿī says the *tashrīq* days are the numbered days, on the evidence of "**whoever hastens in two days**" — [meaning] of the *tashrīq* days — "**there is no sin upon him, and whoever delays, there is no sin upon him**" — the verse specified the numbered days from among the days of pebble-throwing, which are the *tashrīq* days; so the first day, the Day of Sacrifice, is [also] "numbered." And God said in the verse on ḥajj, "**and remember God in numbered days**." As for the "known days" (*al-ayyām al-maʿlūmāt*), for Mālik these are three days after the Day of Sacrifice — so the first day is "numbered" for him but not "known"; the two middle days are both "numbered" and "known"; and the fourth day is "known" but not "numbered." This is Mālik's position.

If the pilgrim throws the pebbles on the two [middle] days and then departs for Mecca, there is no sin on him — this is "hastening." Whoever delays until he completes the three days, there is no sin on him either. [The verse] expresses this by negating sin, because some pilgrims say the one who hastens is sinful, and others say the one who delays is sinful; hence the verse negated sin in both directions. This is [what is meant by] "**for whoever is God-fearing**"

**[170, continuing 169]** — by complying with God's command and avoiding what He has forbidden, for this is the true pilgrim: "Whoever performs ḥajj without obscenity or transgression returns [free] of his sins as on the day his mother bore him," and "There is no reward for an accepted ḥajj (*ḥajj mabrūr*) except Paradise." The sign of an accepted ḥajj is that the pilgrim returns humble, ascetic, turned toward God, Blessed and Exalted. There was a corrupt man who performed the pilgrimage to God's Sacred House and returned, and one day his soul urged him toward some of the sins he used to commit, and he heard [a voice] from within himself: "Would you do this while you are a pilgrim?" — and he was restrained from it. This is the sign of an accepted ḥajj.

"**And know that unto Him you shall be gathered**" — in the hereafter, where He will requite you for your deeds; or [it may mean] you are gathered to Him in any case — the true pilgrim, too, is gathered to the Presence of God, Blessed and Exalted.

"**Among the people is he whose speech about the life of this world impresses you**" — this verse was revealed concerning a man called al-Akhnas ibn Sharīq, a hypocrite of sweet speech toward the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him; he would swear, whenever he was present with the Prophet, that he was a believer in him and loved him, so his company was pleasing — but God, Blessed and Exalted, gave him the lie in this. He passed by some Muslims' crops and donkeys and burned the crops and hamstrung the donkeys by night. When present before the Prophet he would seem a sincere, loving believer, and when he turned away he would become hostile to [the Prophet's] companions and work corruption against them. The Exalted said, "**Among the people is he whose speech about the life of this world impresses you**" — but he will not impress you in the hereafter, on account of his contradicting his own belief — "**and he calls God to witness what is in his heart**," [claiming] that it matches his speech, "**while he is the fiercest of adversaries**" — most severe in hostility toward you and your followers, on account of his enmity for you. This is the third category: the hypocrite who sold his hereafter for his worldly life. "**And when he turns away**" — departs from you — "**he strives**" — walks about — "**in the land to work corruption in it, and to destroy tillage and offspring**" — among other kinds of corruption — "**and God does not love corruption**" — is not pleased by it. "**And when it is said to him, 'Fear God,'**" in your conduct, "**vainglory seizes him in sin**" — arrogance and zealotry drive him to persist in the very sin he was told to guard against — "**so his sufficiency is Hell — an evil resting-place**" indeed.

God does not love corruption, especially not this hypocrite's corruption of tillage and offspring — for when God, Blessed and Exalted, created Adam and sent him down to the earth and made him a vicegerent there, He taught him the craft by which his children would live, and Adam's craft was agriculture. God, Blessed and Exalted, placed blessing in offspring and in tillage; there is more blessing in these than in all other things. If you reflect, you find that most people do not farm — only a few of them occupy themselves with agriculture, and even those who do so are occupied with it only part of the time — and yet you see that no one eats except something that was cultivated. Were it not that God sent down blessing into cultivation, this would not be possible.

And He placed blessing in livestock: sheep, cattle, camels — in most cases these animals give birth only once a year, and when they give birth they bear a single offspring [normally]; if it differs, they bear two. Whoever wants meat must slaughter one of them; whoever wants to sell, sells some of them; and yet one never goes out without encountering a herd of camels, or a herd of cattle, or of sheep. Whereas if you reflect on predators, on dogs, even on cats, you find they give birth twice a year, each time bearing eight young — yet people are not occupied with slaughtering or selling them, and you never see many of them at all; if you encountered five dogs you would say, "That's a lot of dogs" — yet you find a hundred cattle, and you find a hundred sheep. Were it not that blessing had come down upon livestock, this would not be so. Hence this hypocrite works against God, Blessed and Exalted, by busying himself with the destruction of tillage and livestock. "**And when it is said to him, 'Fear God,' vainglory seizes him**" — this is arrogance at its extreme: whoever is told "Fear God" and does not humble himself is arrogant — hence when ʿUmar was told "Fear God," he, may God be pleased with him, pressed his cheek to the ground in humility before God, the Exalted.

**[171]** "**And among the people is he who sells himself**" — this is Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī, the Companion of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him. He was in Mecca, and when the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, emigrated, Ṣuhayb set out to emigrate with eight others; the disbelievers intercepted them and killed all his companions until only he remained. They confronted him to prevent his emigration. He had with him a quiver full of arrows, and he said to them: "O people, you know that I am a marksman and this quiver of mine is full of arrows. If you wish to come between me and my emigration to the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, I will empty my quiver into you, and with every arrow I will kill one of you, and when what is in my quiver runs out I will fight you with my sword until I die — do after that as you wish. But it is better for you that I give you my wealth that I left in Mecca, and you let me go to the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him." They agreed to that. When he reached the outskirts of Medina, Abū Bakr met him and said to him: "The transaction has profited!" He said: "What do you mean?" [Abū Bakr] said: "A verse was revealed concerning you," and recited to him this verse: "**And among the people is he who sells himself**" — offers it up in obedience to God — "**seeking the good pleasure of God**" — His pleasure. This is Ṣuhayb, who emigrated to Medina and left them his wealth. "**And God is compassionate to His servants**" — in that He guided them to what pleases Him. This is the fourth category: he who sold his worldly life for his hereafter.

The [ordinary] Muslim sells himself by his own choice to God, Blessed and Exalted, and its price is Paradise; the elect sell themselves by their own choice to God, Blessed and Exalted, and its price is God's good pleasure, Blessed and Exalted; and the common believers' price for themselves is Paradise: "**God has purchased from the believers their selves and their wealth, in that Paradise shall be theirs**" — whereas the elect's price for themselves is God's good pleasure, Blessed and Exalted: "**And among the people is he who sells himself**" — that is, offers it up — "**seeking the good pleasure of God — and God is compassionate to His servants**." The servant must offer his wealth to God, his children to God, and his own self to God. Whoever offers his wealth to God finds annihilation (*fanāʾ*) in [divine] acts, knowing that there is no agent in existence but God — annihilation in acts is the price of one who offers his wealth to God. And whoever surrenders his children to God finds annihilation in the attributes of God, Blessed and Exalted. And whoever surrenders his own self to God finds annihilation in the Essence of God, Blessed and Exalted, so he is annihilated from himself and subsists (*yabqā*) through God — "**and that unto your Lord is the final end**." "**And God is compassionate to His servants.**"

"**O you who believe, enter wholly into submission [Islam]**" — this verse was revealed concerning ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his companions, who upon entering Islam continued to venerate the Sabbath and to dislike camel meat and camel milk, in keeping with their former religion; so it was revealed concerning them: "**O you who believe, enter into submission**" — into Islam — "**wholly**" — that is, in all its statutes — "**and do not follow the footsteps of Satan**" — the paths of Satan, his adorning of division — "**for he is to you a manifest enemy**" — openly hostile. So they returned and began doing everything the [other] Muslims did. This still applies today to whoever follows certain customs and holds to racial division after Islam has said, "**The believers are but brothers**," while abandoning other things that are harmless, merely in deference to blameworthy customs.

"**Enter into submission wholly**" — this address encompasses all Muslims, that they should enter Islam wholly, in all its parts. It is explained by the ḥadīth: "The Muslim is he from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe" — or it is an address to every person that he should bring all his limbs into Islam: he enters Islam with the totality of his limbs, so that he walks only to what God has commanded, strikes with his hands only by God's command, looks with his eyes only by God's command, speaks with his tongue, eats with his stomach, and satisfies the desire of his loins only in accordance with the command of God, Blessed and Exalted — this is the entry into Islam, wholly. This is the entry of the self (*al-nafs*); the heart divests itself of all blameworthy character-traits and adorns itself with all praiseworthy ones; the spirit (*al-rūḥ*) abandons attending to anything but God, Blessed and Exalted; and the innermost secret (*al-sirr*) is annihilated in the Essence of God, Blessed and Exalted, seeing nothing else — this is the entry into Islam, wholly.

"**But if you slip**" — deviate from entering wholly — "**after clear proofs have come to you**" — the manifest arguments for its truth — "**then know that God is Mighty**" — nothing can render Him incapable of taking vengeance on you — "**Wise**" in His making. "**What do they await**" — what do those who abandon entering Islam wholly wait for — "**but that God should come to them**" — God's command — "**in canopies**" — plural of canopy — "**of clouds, and the angels, and the matter be decided**" — their destruction accomplished — "**and to God all matters return**," so that He requites every servant for his deed — good for good, evil for evil.

"**Ask**," O Muḥammad, "**the Children of Israel**" — as a rebuke — "**how many clear signs We gave them**" — how many did they find, of the manifest signs, such as the splitting of the sea and the sending down of manna and quails — and all these signs they turned into disbelief. "**And whoever exchanges God's favor**" — what He favored him with, of signs, since they are a cause of guidance — "**after it has come to him**" — for disbelief — "**then indeed God is severe in punishment**" toward him.

"**Adorned is [the life of this world] for those who disbelieve**" — this was revealed concerning the disbelievers of Mecca, who used to mock the poor among the Muslims and deride them, such as Bilāl, ʿAmmār, and Ṣuhayb. So the Exalted said, "**Adorned is**" for those Meccan disbelievers "**the life of this world**" — through deceptive appeal, so they loved it; they loved this world and saw it as everything — when they found wealth they found what they loved and supposed that everything was thereby complete for them, so they gave no thought to their hereafter or to their Master. "**And they mock**" — by that they mock — "**those who believe**" — for their poverty; and they mock the poor Muslims, "**and**" — [also mock] "**those who fear [God]**" — those who feared associating partners with God, and these are the [believers] — "**shall be above them on the Day of Resurrection**": and these paupers whom they mock and laugh at will be above them on the Day of Resurrection, in the ranks of Paradise, while they [the mockers] are in the lowest depth of the Fire. "**And God provides for whom He wills without reckoning**" — abundant provision in the hereafter, unlike this world, where everything is limited and bounded — God, Blessed and Exalted, provides for His friends without reckoning.

God mentions in this verse that the God-fearing will be above the [other] people on the Day of Resurrection, and whoever desires this elevation should follow the example of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, in renouncing this world and what is in it. The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, was offered the treasuries of the earth, the fifths of the spoils were brought to him, and kings east and west gifted him vast wealth, yet he kept not a single dirham of it for himself — rather he enriched others and strengthened the Muslims with what God gave him, and gave up his own share, until he died, peace and blessings upon him, with his coat of mail pawned to a Jew for the provisioning of his household. Whoever seeks this elevation must renounce this world, turn toward God, Blessed and Exalted, choose what is with God, and hold it dearer than what is with people.

"**Mankind was one community**" — before the sending of the prophets, people were of one community, one religion, namely faith (*īmān*), and then they differed, some believing and some disbelieving — "**so God raised up the prophets**" to them, "**bringing good tidings**" to those who believed, [of] Paradise, "**and warning**" those who disbelieved, [of] the Fire, "**and sent down with them the Book**" — the scriptures — "**in truth, to judge between the people concerning that in which they differed**" regarding religion. "**And none differed concerning it**" — religion — "**except those who were given it**" — the Book — so some believed and some disbelieved, "**out of envy among themselves, after clear proofs had come to them**" — the manifest arguments for monotheism. "**So God guided those who believed to the truth concerning that over which they had differed, by His leave**" — this phrase involves inversion, an anticipation and postponement [of grammatical elements]: God only guided those who believed away from disagreement toward the truth — He did not guide them from the truth toward disagreement, but rather from disagreement toward the truth. The *lām* stands in the place of *min* and *min* in the place of *lām* — this occurs in Arabic speech, and the Qur'an was revealed in Arabic; everything the Arabs articulate, the Qur'an articulates. "**And God guides whom He wills to a straight path**" — the path of truth.

God, Blessed and Exalted, took the covenant upon the offspring of Adam: "**Am I not your Lord? They said, Yes**" — so every one of them is born upon this faith. Hence the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "Every newborn is born upon the [innate] disposition (*fiṭra*); it is his parents who make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Zoroastrian" — and he did not say "make him a Muslim." A human being is born upon Islam; if he disbelieves into Christianity or Judaism, he is an apostate, whereas if he remains upon Islam he has remained upon the innate disposition: "**[This is] the disposition of God upon which He created mankind — there is no altering God's creation.**"

**[177, continuing 169]** "**Or did you suppose that you would enter Paradise**" — this was revealed concerning hardship that befell the Muslims. The Muslims, at the beginning of their affair, were subjected to all kinds of harm; the disbelievers confronted them with every injury, until the Prophet ordered them to emigrate to Abyssinia, and after that the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, himself emigrated with his own noble person to Medina the Illuminated. And the disbelievers never ceased harming the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, and his companions — and the worst they suffered from the disbelievers was the Day of the Trench: the disbelievers called to one another from every direction against Medina, while [the Muslims] were neighbors to their enemies the Jews, and the hypocrites were among them, so they suffered severe injury from the Jews. The Qur'an described them: "**and hearts reached the throats**." So the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, prayed, and God relieved them, and he said: "Those before you would be persecuted for their religion — a saw would be placed on one's head and he would be sawn until split into two halves, and that would not turn him from his religion; and one of them would have an iron comb applied that would separate his flesh from his bones until his skin was flayed while he was alive, and that would not turn him from his religion. By God, God will complete this affair until the rider travels from Ṣanʿāʾ to Ḥaḍramawt fearing none but God and the wolf for his flock" — and every messenger God sent was harmed, and his people were made to suffer harm, until it was said, until God's help [came] — even to the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, and his companions.

"**Or did you suppose that you would enter Paradise without there having come to you the like of**" — the similitude of what befell — "**those who passed away before you**," of the believers, of trials, so that you might be patient as they were patient — "**Affliction and adversity befell them**" — severe poverty — "**and hardship**" — sickness — "**and they were shaken**" — terrified by all kinds of trial — "**until the Messenger, and those who believed with him, said**" — out of impatience at how prolonged the affliction was — "**'When [will] the help of God [come]?'**" When will God's promised help arrive? So they were answered on God's behalf: "**Indeed, the help of God is near**" — its arrival is imminent. This is God's [immutable] way, Blessed and Exalted — "**and you will never find any alteration in God's way**."

"**They ask you what they should spend**" — the questioner was ʿAmr ibn al-Jamūḥ, an elderly man of wealth, who asked the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, what he should spend and on whom he should spend it. "**They ask you**," O Muḥammad, "**what they should spend**" — that is, what they ought to give — "**Say**" to them, "**Whatever good you spend**" — of any wealth, little or much — this clarifies the object spent, which is what the questioner asked about — "**it is for parents**" — He answered concerning the recipient, which is the other half [of the question] — "**and relatives, and orphans, and the poor, and the wayfarer**." A person spends of his wealth, little or much, as he wishes, but the recipients of charitable spending — parents, relatives, orphans, the poor, and the wayfarer — have first claim to it. "**And whatever good you do, God is aware of it**" — spending or otherwise — so He requites for it. And "spending" (*infāq*) covers everything a man gives: the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, says: "Do not disdain any good deed, even pouring from your bucket into your fellow water-carrier's vessel, or meeting your brother with a cheerful face" — all these are acts of charity — "or striving alongside him in his need, whether it is fulfilled or not" — all these are acts of charity. "**And whatever good you do, God is aware of it**," so He requites for it.

**[178]** "**Fighting has been prescribed**" — made obligatory — "**for you**," against the disbelievers, "**though it is hateful**" — disliked — "**to you**" — naturally, on account of its hardship. When God made jihad obligatory upon us, this obligation weighed heavily on the souls of the Muslims. "**But it may be that you dislike a thing though it is good for you, and it may be that you love a thing though it is bad for you**" — on account of the soul's inclination toward the appetites that lead to its ruin, and its aversion to the obligations that lead to its felicity. So perhaps for you, in fighting, if you dislike it, there is good — either triumph and spoils, or martyrdom and reward — while in forsaking it, even if you love [to forsake it], there is evil — humiliation, poverty, and deprivation of reward. "**And God knows**" what is good for you, "**while you do not know**" — so hasten to what He commands you.

"**They ask you concerning the sacred month, fighting in it**" — the occasion of this verse's revelation: the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, sent his first raiding-party under ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaḥsh, eight men with him; they fought the polytheists and killed a son of al-Ḥaḍramī on the last day of Jumādā al-Ākhira, and the timing had become uncertain for them, so that Rajab had begun — and the disbelievers reproached them for treating it as licit.

The Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, had sent, two months before Badr, eight of his companions, and wrote for their commander ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaḥsh a letter and sealed it, and wrote him another letter, saying to him: "Set out in the name of God, upon God's blessing, and do not open this letter until you have traveled two days; then when you have traveled two days, open the letter, and do not compel anyone to march with you — whoever wishes to travel with you may travel, and whoever wishes to turn back may turn back." When he had traveled two days he opened the letter and found in it: "In the name of God, proceed upon God's blessing until you reach Baṭn Nakhla" — a place between al-Ṭāʾif and Mecca — "and if you encounter a Quraysh caravan, perhaps you will bring us some good from them." So they proceeded until they reached Baṭn Nakhla on the last day of Jumādā al-Ākhira, before Rajab — which is one of the sacred months, in which fighting [is forbidden]. When the disbelievers saw them, ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaḥsh said to his companions: "Shave the head of one of you" — so they shaved one man's head. When [the enemy] saw him shaven-headed, they said, "These are a people who have come only for ʿumra," and so they felt safe — whereupon the Companions fell upon them; Wāqid shot ʿAmr ibn al-Ḥaḍramī and he fell dead — the first of the polytheists to be killed, and this [Wāqid] was the first to kill in the path of God. Nawfal fled, and they took ʿUthmān ibn ʿAbd Allāh and al-Ḥakam ibn Kaysān captive, and seized two camels and much baggage as spoils, and brought it to Medina.

So the disbelievers of Quraysh reproached the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, saying to him: "You fight in the sacred territory and in the sacred months," and they went about reproaching the Muslims who were [still] in Mecca: "This messenger of yours, O Companions, has now begun treating the sacred months as licit, which God, Blessed and Exalted, has forbidden." So the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, said, "I did not command them to fight in the sacred months." Then this verse was revealed: "**They ask you concerning the sacred month**" — al-Muḥarram — "**fighting in it**" — in place of, "**Say, fighting in it is grave**" — enormous, on account of what results from it, "**and [it is grave to cause] hindrance**" — preventing people — "**from the path of God**" — His religion, which is their [the believers'] work — "**and disbelief in Him**" — disbelief in God, Blessed and Exalted — "**and**" hindrance from — "**the Sacred Mosque, and expelling its people from it**" — meaning the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, and the believers with him, they are the people of the Mosque, and [the disbelievers] expelled them from Mecca — this "**is graver**" — a greater sin — "**with God**," Blessed and Exalted, "**than fighting [in the sacred month]. And persecution**" — associating partners with God on your part — "**is graver than killing**" [on ours]. "**And they will not cease**" — the disbelievers — "**to fight you**," O believers, "**until they turn you back from your religion**" to disbelief, "**if they are able**."

**[180]** This is their perpetual practice, never to cease: the disbelievers will not stop fighting the believers to turn them back to disbelief, whenever they are able to do so.

"**And whoever among you turns back from his religion and dies while a disbeliever, those are the ones whose deeds have come to nothing**" — been nullified — "**in this world and the hereafter**" — no credit for them nor reward upon them. The condition of dying [an apostate] indicates that had he returned to Islam, his [prior good] deeds would not be nullified, so he would be rewarded for them and would not need to repeat them — the ḥajj, for instance — and this is al-Shāfiʿī's position. When this verse was revealed the Muslims rejoiced, knowing they were absolved [of wrongdoing] by the Prophet regarding their killing [in the sacred month]; but they had no [immediate] claim on the reward [for the spoils], and they held back the spoils. "**And those are the companions of the Fire; they abide therein forever**" — the apostates.

It was revealed: "**Indeed those who believed, and those who emigrated**" — left their homelands — "**and strove in the path of God**" — to exalt His religion — "**those hope for the mercy of God**" — God's reward — "**and God is forgiving**" toward the believers, "**merciful**." And the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, held back the spoils, taking the fifth for himself and dividing the rest among those who were present at the raid.

God, Blessed and Exalted, described the believers by emigration, and emigration is of two kinds: emigration from the land of disbelief to the land of Islam, which ended with the conquest of Mecca ("there is no emigration after the conquest"); and emigration from the homelands of the [lower] self, from carnal appetites, to the worship of God, Blessed and Exalted — and this never ends. And jihad is of two kinds: the lesser jihad and the greater jihad. The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "We have returned from the lesser jihad to the greater jihad." They said: "Is striking with swords the lesser jihad?" He said: "Yes — and the greater jihad is the struggle against the self, caprice, and Satan" — for the fighter in the first arena's utmost end is to be killed by the sword

**[181]** and become a martyr, while the fighter in the second arena's utmost end is to be annihilated in the Essence of God, Blessed and Exalted, becoming a gnostic, a *ṣiddīq* — and the *ṣiddīqūn* rank higher than the martyrs, on the evidence of the verse "**among the prophets, the truthful (ṣiddīqīn), the martyrs, and the righteous**," where the truthful are mentioned before the martyrs. And emigration is of two kinds: emigration from one land to another, and emigration from the accustomed habits of the self to what the Lord, Glorified and Exalted, loves.

"**They ask you concerning wine and games of chance**" — four verses were revealed concerning wine. "**And of the fruits of the date-palms and grapevines you take an intoxicant and goodly provision**" — this is the first thing revealed concerning wine, and this verse praised wine — "**an intoxicant and goodly provision**" — so the Muslims took it as permissible to drink wine, and they drank, it being lawful, until ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may God be pleased with him, asked the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him: "O Messenger of God, ask God to give us a clear ruling concerning wine — this wine clouds the mind and brings about corruptions, and this game of chance in which a person finds abundant wealth without effort — ask God, Blessed and Exalted, to make clear to us the ruling on this." So the second verse was revealed: "**They ask you concerning wine and games of chance**" — what is their ruling — "**Say, in them both is great sin**" — enormous, on account of the quarreling, cursing, and obscenity that result from them — "**and some benefits for people**" — pleasure and joy in wine, and the acquisition of wealth without toil in gambling. When this verse was revealed, some of the Companions drank wine and others abstained from it; some said "some benefits for people" means it should be taken for its benefits, while others said "in them both is great sin," meaning the great sin has no benefit in it. "**And their sin is greater than their benefit**" — on account of the corruptions that arise from them. When this was revealed, some people drank and others abstained, so ʿUmar said: "O God, make clear to us a satisfying clarification concerning wine."

Then ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, who was among the wealthy Companions, held a banquet for the Companions; they ate and drank wine, and when the sunset prayer time came they put one of them forward and he was

**[182]** drunk, and he recited Sūrat al-Kāfirūn and dropped all the negations from it. So ʿUmar said: "O God, make clear to us a satisfying clarification concerning wine." Then it was revealed: "**Do not approach the prayer while you are intoxicated, until you know what you are saying**." Some said: it is forbidden [absolutely]; others said, no, it is forbidden only during prayer times — so some would abstain from wine upon rising, so as to pray the five prayers, and afterward take wine and spend the night drunk, and when morning came they would be sober and would pray. So ʿUmar said: "O God, make clear to us a satisfying clarification concerning wine." Then it was revealed: "**O you who believe, wine, games of chance, [sacrificial] stones, and divining arrows are but defilement, of Satan's doing; so avoid it, that you may prosper. Satan only wishes to cast enmity and hatred between you through wine and games of chance, and to bar you from the remembrance of God and from prayer — so will you not desist?**" So ʿUmar said: "We desist! O God, we desist!" So wine was forbidden from that day, and its prohibition became severe — the most severe of prohibitions is the prohibition of wine. Hence ʿAlī, may God ennoble his countenance, said: "If wine were poured into a well and a minaret built over it, I would not give the call to prayer atop it; and if it were poured

**[183]** into the sea, so that the sea dried up and grass grew, I would not let my livestock graze on it." Gabriel told the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, that God had praised four qualities in Jaʿfar al-Ṭayyār, so the Messenger asked Jaʿfar, saying to him: "What are the four qualities God praised in you from the Age of Ignorance?" He said: "By God, O Messenger of God, had God not told you, I would not have told you myself: I never drank wine at all in the Age of Ignorance, because I have more need of what increases my reason than of what removes it; second, I never worshipped an idol at all, because I saw that it neither harms nor benefits; third, I never committed fornication at all in the Age of Ignorance, because I am jealous and do not love anyone touching my women or my daughters, and what I would not love done to me, I do not do to another; and fourth, I never lied at all, because I saw that lying is base."

"**And they ask you what they should spend**" — how much — "**Say**" to them, spend "**the surplus**" — that is, what is beyond need, and do not spend what you yourselves need, thereby ruining yourselves. The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, says: "Begin with yourself, then with whoever is dependent on you, and the best charity is what is given out of surplus wealth (*ẓahr ghinā*)." And yet Abū Bakr gave in charity the whole

**[184]** of his wealth, and the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, endorsed it. One day the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, called for spending in the path of God. ʿUmar said: it matched wealth I happened to have — I found I had eight thousand dinars. I said: every good deed that has come, Abū Bakr has outstripped me to it; today, though, [I shall not let this pass] — half of what I have, I brought to the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, and said to him: I had eight thousand dinars; four I have put in the path of God and four I have kept for my dependents, so this shall be counted for me with God, Blessed and Exalted. Then Abū Bakr came and said: "All the wealth I have is charity in the path of God." The Messenger said: "What have you left for your family?" He said: "God and His Messenger suffice them." The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "Between the two of you is the difference between your two sayings." This one said he kept back half of what he had for his family, while Abū Bakr said, "God and His Messenger suffice them." ʿUmar said: "By God, I will never race you again after this day." This is the proof of Abū Bakr's excellence over ʿUmar; but excellence in every respect belonged to none but the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him — as for anyone other than the Messenger, if he excels in one respect he is surpassed in another. Abū Bakr's predominant state was gnosis (*maʿrifa*); ʿUmar's predominant state was the [outward] Law (*sharīʿa*); ʿUthmān's predominant [state] was the [spiritual] Path (*ṭarīqa*); and ʿAlī's predominant [state] was the Reality (*ḥaqīqa*) — though each of them was complete in all four matters. Hence Abū Bakr is the most excellent of the Companions, though some Companions surpass him in some particular things.

**[185]** And you find people vary: you find this one complete in this respect, deficient in that respect, superior in this — and the avenues of good are many; whatever suits a person, that is his door. You find a servant of God to whom God has opened a door in prayer but not in fasting; one opened to him in fasting but not in the remembrance of God; one opened to him in remembrance but not in almsgiving; one opened to him in almsgiving but not in recitation. Whatever door God opens for you, hold fast to that alone, and you will find the good in it — God loves from the servant simply that he do good: "**and do good, that you may prosper**." And likewise, just as He has made clear to you what has been mentioned, "**God makes clear to you the signs, that you may reflect**"

"**on this world and the hereafter**" — God made every sign clear so that the servant would reflect in every circumstance, in this world and the next, and so take up what is most beneficial for him in both. God, Blessed and Exalted — His signs are of [several] kinds: dazzling signs, like the miracles of the prophets; signs that break the ordinary course of things, like thunder, lightning, and the descent of rain — whenever any of these recurs for the servant he knows it is a sign among God's signs — and the eclipse of the sun and moon, and so on; and a sign in every atom — you find no atom in existence but that it is a sign among the signs of God, Blessed and Exalted.

*[And in everything there is a sign for Him, indicating that He is One.]*

**[186]** The intelligent person sees nothing without seeing in it a sign, and reflecting on this world and the hereafter — for this world is the title-page of the hereafter, and the outward is the title-page of the inward. So if you see something beautiful, it reminds you of the wide-eyed maidens [of Paradise] and what is in Paradise of bliss, or reminds you of something higher than that — the beauty of God, Blessed and Exalted, and the countenance of His Messenger, peace and blessings upon him. And if you see something ugly, you recall Munkar and Nakīr; and if you hear a rejection, you recall rejection before God, Blessed and Exalted; and if you see acceptance, it reminds you of acceptance before God, Blessed and Exalted; and if you see [someone's] approach, it reminds you of God's approach toward you; and if you see [someone's] aversion, it reminds you of God's aversion from you — so you seek refuge in God from that. Everything you see outwardly is the title-page of what is with God, Blessed and Exalted, inwardly. Hence He said in this verse that He has made clear to us the signs in all their aspects, that we might reflect upon this world and the hereafter.

"**And they ask you concerning orphans**" — and the difficulty they encounter regarding them — when the verse was revealed, "**Those who consume the wealth of orphans wrongfully only consume fire into their bellies, and they will burn in a blaze**," they became anxious about the matter of orphans, saying: what should we do with the orphans' wealth? Shall we mix their wealth with ours and thereby fall into difficulty, or set their wealth apart — but if we set it apart it will be wasted, and how shall we provide them food? — and so they grew anxious about the matter of orphans. The Exalted said, "**And they ask you concerning orphans**" — "**Say, setting things right for them**" — in their wealth, by increasing it and mingling your affairs with theirs — "**is better**" than leaving that be — "**and if you mix your affairs with theirs, they are your brothers**" — they are your brothers in religion, and it is the way of a brother to mingle his affairs with his brother's, so this is permitted to you: a person may place his orphan ward's wealth within his own and keep it for him until, when the ward reaches maturity, he hands over his wealth to him, taking from it his own provisioning and clothing and reckoning it against him, and what remains he hands over to him when he has grown strong and reached maturity — this is what is most proper concerning orphans. "**And God knows the one who corrupts**" — their wealth through commingling; whoever intends corruption, God knows it — "**from the one who sets right**" — and whoever intends to set right, God knows that too, and requites each of them accordingly — "**and had God willed, He could have caused you hardship**" — by narrowing things upon you through forbidding commingling altogether — "**Indeed God is Mighty**" — prevailing in His command — "**Wise**" in His making.

**[187]** It is related, on the merits of spending on the orphan, the Messenger's saying, peace and blessings upon him: "Whoever prepares food and brings an orphan to share it earns Paradise," and his saying: "I and the guardian of the orphan will be like these two in Paradise" — indicating with his index and middle fingers. And how should a person act if he is guardian to an orphan — should he discipline him by striking, or not, given the severe warning that has come regarding striking an orphan? But this [warning] does not concern striking for the sake of the child's upbringing and correction — for a blow may be better for the orphan than a sweet morsel he eats [instead]. So whoever strikes him for discipline and to set him aright commits no wrong and no sin, while whoever strikes an orphan merely on account of his weakness — that one is the wrongdoer.

"**And do not marry idolatresses until they believe**" — occasion of revelation: one of the Companions had married a slave-woman — this was ʿAbd Allāh ibn Rawāḥa, and Ḥudhayfa ibn al-Yamān, each of whom owned a slave-woman, freed her, and married her — and he was reproached for it: why did he not marry a free woman of equal standing? "**And do not marry idolatresses**" — disbelieving women — "**until they believe**" — it is forbidden for a Muslim [man] to marry a disbelieving woman — "**and a believing slave-woman is better than an idolatress**," free woman, "**even if she pleases you**" for her beauty and wealth — this is specific to non-scriptuary women, [excepted] by the verse "**and the chaste women among those given the Book**," which was revealed at the end of the last sūra to be revealed. Whoever wishes to marry should choose a believing Muslim woman; the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, says: "A woman is married for four reasons: for her wealth, her lineage,

**[188]** her beauty, and her religion — so secure the one of religion, may your hands be dusted [with earning her]!" Whoever wishes to marry should look to the one possessed of religion, even if plain in appearance — she is better than a beautiful woman without religion; and even if she is owned or a freedwoman, if she is devout she is better than a wealthy woman of good lineage without religion.

There was a righteous man who had an excellent daughter, and men sought her hand from every quarter. He had a black servant named Mubārak, the father of ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Mubārak, so he called him and said to him: "I wish to consult you — my daughter has reached marriageable age and men are seeking her hand, and I would like your opinion on the matter." The servant said to him: "You are of the Arabs — the Arabs choose noble lineage; the people of your time choose wealth; and your Prophet chooses religion alone." So [the father] said: "I choose what the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, chose — and I have not seen anyone of better religion than you; I hereby marry her to you." This is the father of ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Mubārak, who filled the horizons with knowledge.

"**And do not marry idolaters until they believe**" — disbelieving men — "**and a believing slave is better than an idolater, even if he pleases you**" with his wealth and his looks — "**those call to the Fire**" — by calling to what necessitates it, so marrying them is unfitting — "**while God calls**" — through the tongues of His messengers — "**to Paradise and forgiveness**" — the acts that necessitate them — "**by His leave**" — by His will, so it is obligatory to answer Him by marrying His close ones — "**and He makes clear His signs to the people, that they may take heed**" — be admonished.

"**And they ask you concerning menstruation**" — occasion of revelation: when a woman menstruated they used to isolate her, expelling her from the house; and some did not avoid the woman [at all] during her menses — so they asked the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, "**And they ask you concerning menstruation**" — the menses, or its site — what is to be done with women during it. "**Say, it**" — menstruation — "**is a hurt**" — a filth —

**[189]** "**so keep away from women**" — that is, abstain from intercourse with them — "**during menstruation, and do not approach them**" — sexually — "**until they are purified**" — until they have washed after it has ended — "**but when they have purified themselves, come to them**" — sexually — "**from where God has commanded you**" — avoiding it during menstruation, that is, [confining yourselves to] the front, and do not go beyond that to elsewhere. "**Indeed God loves**" — rewards and honors — "**those who turn to Him repeatedly**" of sins, "**and loves those who purify themselves**" of impurities. "**Your women are a tillage for you**" — they asked the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, about a saying of the Jews: the Jews used to tell the Muslims that whoever approached his wife in the front passage from the rear would produce a cross-eyed or foolish child. So they asked the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, about that. "**Your women**" — your wives — "**are a tillage for you**" — merely the place of your sowing, since it is the place where offspring are sown — "**so come to your tillage**" — its place, which is the front — "**however you wish**" — standing, sitting, lying down, from the front or from behind — all this refutes the Jews' claim that approaching one's wife's front from behind produces a cross-eyed child. The sunna manner is for the woman to lie on her back and the man to come to her from above, as the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, used to do.

"**And send ahead for yourselves**" — righteous deeds, such as the invocation at the time of intercourse: "In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate. O God, keep Satan away from us and keep Satan away from what You provide us" — whoever says this at the time of intercourse, should God decree a child, it will be protected from Satan, and it is recorded for its father, for every breath [of the child's life], a good deed — and a single good deed is better than this world and everything in it. Hence He said, "**And send ahead for yourselves**" — the righteous deed, namely the invocation at intercourse — "**and fear God**" in His command and prohibition,

**[190]** "**and know that you shall meet Him**" — through the resurrection, when He will requite you for your deeds — "**and give good tidings to the believers**" — those who feared [God] — give them good tidings of Paradise. "**And do not make God**" — the oath by God — "**an obstruction**" — a preventing cause — "**to your oaths, that you should act righteously**" — that you should not act righteously — "**and be God-fearing**" — one of them had sworn not to do something, and afterward, wishing to return to what he had been doing, he said: "The oath prevents me, since I swore by God" — so this verse was revealed: do not make the oath a pretext preventing you from doing good — "**and make peace between people. And God is All-Hearing**" of your words, "**All-Knowing**" of your states. "**God will not take you to task for what is unintentional in your oaths**" — that which the tongue utters unintentionally — according to the Shāfiʿīs: [saying] "No, by God" or "Yes, by God" [as a verbal habit]; and according to the Mālikīs: one who was certain of something and swore upon it, and it then turned out otherwise. "**But He will take you to task for what your hearts have earned**" — what you intended by the oaths, if you break them — "**and God is Forgiving**" of what was unintentional, "**Forbearing**" in delaying the punishment from those who deserve it. "**For those who swear off from their wives**" — swear not to have intercourse with them — "**a waiting period of four months**" — awaiting four months — "**and if they return**" — wish to return to intercourse after

**[191]** the oath — "**then indeed God is Forgiving, Merciful**" — "Forgiving" of whatever harm they caused the wife through the oath, "Merciful" toward them, in that if they wish to return they need only pay the expiation, and their wives become lawful to them again. Whoever swears he will not have intercourse with his wife for the duration of his life is held to four months; after four months, either he must expiate his oath or his wife is divorced from him — this applies to every kind of oath. As for the "stricter" [distinction] some jurists speak of, I myself have not recognized it — it is not in the Book nor in the Sunna. "**And if they resolve on divorce**" — by not returning — let them carry it out — "**then indeed God is All-Hearing**" of their words, "**All-Knowing**" of their resolve; after the waiting period mentioned, there is nothing but return or divorce. "**And divorced women shall wait**" — let them wait — "**by themselves**" — from [re]marriage — "**for three menstrual courses**" — passing from the time of divorce, once she menstruates and becomes pure, and menstruates and becomes pure, and menstruates and becomes pure — these are the three *qurūʾ*.

**[192]** "**And it is not lawful for them to conceal what God has created in their wombs**" — of child or of menstruation — "**if they believe in God and the Last Day. And their husbands have greater right to take them back**" — by resuming the marriage, even if [the wives] are unwilling — "**in that [period]**" — during the waiting period — "**if they desire reconciliation**" — between the two of them, not to harm the woman; this is an encouragement toward that intent, not a condition for the permissibility of return. "**And they [the women] have rights**" over their husbands "**similar to those [obligations] upon them**" — the woman has a claim upon the man just as the man has a claim upon the woman — "**in accordance with what is fitting**" — legally, in good companionship and the avoidance of harm and the like — "**and men have a degree above them**" — a merit in right, on account of the obligation of their obedience to the men, for what the men have provided of dower and maintenance — "**and God is Mighty**" in His dominion, "**Wise**" in what He has ordained for His creation.

God, Blessed and Exalted, commanded good companionship with women. The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, says: There is no good in women, nor patience without them — "I have never seen [any group] more deficient in reason and religion able to sweep away the resolve of a determined man than one of you [women]!" — if you live with them, consult them, for good companionship, and oppose them [in independent judgment], because they are deficient in reason. There was, in the time of one of the prophets, a man who asked that prophet of that age to pray

**[193]** to God for him. So the prophet, peace and blessings upon him, prayed to God, and God said to His prophet: Tell so-and-so, I have granted him three requests, whatever he wishes — three things he shall have as he wishes. He said: let me consult my wife. So he went and said to her: We used to ask something of God, and today the request has come from God — He has granted me three requests to ask of Him. The woman said: The first, that He make me the most beautiful woman in the world. He prayed, and she became the most beautiful woman in the world — her beauty filled the house. She got up preparing herself. He said to her: Where to? She said: To a king who suits me. He said to her, imploring her: do not do this — and he pressed her, but she insisted on going to the king of that time. So he said: O God, the second request, that she be transformed into a monkey — and she turned into a monkey. Two requests were lost, and she began to weep. He said: What is your view now? She said: Ask God to return me to my state, and I will stay with you. So he asked God, and she returned to her state and stayed with him. All the requests were lost — he gained nothing, on account of his wife's folly.

"**Divorce is [revocable] twice**" — the divorce after which reconciliation is possible — whoever divorces a wife may marry her again a second time, and if he divorces her a second time he may marry her again.

[A man] said to his wife: I will neither divorce you [finally] nor shelter you. She said: How so? He said: I will divorce you, and when your waiting period nears its end, I will take you back [and divorce you again, endlessly]. So she came to the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, and mentioned this to him, and God, Mighty and Exalted, sent down: "**Divorce is [revocable] twice**"

**[193/194]** "**then either retention**" — you must retain them, after this — after the second divorce there is nothing but retention — "**in accordance with what is fitting**" — without harm — "**or release**" — letting them go — "**with kindness**" — should there be a further divorce, she is released outright. "**And it is not lawful for you**," O husbands, "**to take back anything of what you have given them**" — of dowries — it is not lawful for a man to divorce a woman and take back anything of the property he had given her before, "**except when both fear**" — the spouses — "**that they will not be able to keep within the limits set by God**" — unless the cause of divorce is that they both fear they cannot uphold God's limits — "**then if you fear that they will not be able to keep within the limits of God, there is no blame upon either of them for what she gives up to secure her release**" — [this applies] if the husband does not wish to divorce his wife, but the wife rebels and refuses to remain with him, so that it becomes clear that the two of them are unable to uphold God's limits while remaining together — then *khulʿ* (redemptive divorce) is permitted: the woman may ransom herself from her husband with money, whether the [returned] dower or more or less — this is the basis of *khulʿ*.

A Companion-woman, who was righteous, came to the Prophet, peace and blessings upon him, and said to him: "O Messenger of God, I hold nothing against my husband in religion or otherwise, and he fulfills what is obligatory upon him, except that I no longer love him." He said to her: "What is the reason for that?" She said: "Nothing, except that I saw him approaching with his companions, and I looked at him, and he was the shortest and ugliest of them in face — though I have seen nothing objectionable in him except that I cannot remain with him." The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "Shall I give him a dower on your behalf?" She said: "Yes, give him back my date-orchard." He said: "Will you return his orchard to him?" She said: "Yes, and more besides." He, peace and blessings upon him, said: "Return his orchard to him" — so she returned the orchard to her husband and was divorced. This is the basis for *khulʿ*.

"**These**" — the rulings mentioned — "**are the limits of God, so do not transgress them; and whoever transgresses the limits of

**[195]** God, those are the wrongdoers. So if he divorces her**" — the husband, after the two [prior divorces] — "**she is not lawful to him thereafter**" — the third divorce — "**until she marries**" — weds — "**another husband**" and he consummates [the marriage] with her, as in the ḥadīth reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim: it is related that a beautiful Companion-woman came to the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, and said: "My husband divorced me finally three times, and I married another man, and I found he was unable to consummate with me, for what he has is like a fringe of a garment." The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "Do you wish to return to the husband who divorced you finally? No — not until you taste his sweetness (*ʿusayla*) and he tastes yours" — meaning intercourse. So she came the next day and said to the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him: "What you told me happened — he tasted of me and I tasted of him." The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "Your first statement was false, that what he has is like a fringe of a garment" — and so he barred her from returning to her first husband. And the second husband, whom she claimed was unable to consummate, said: "O Messenger of God, how she lied — I go at her vigorously." And he [subsequently] fathered two sons by her. The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: "These two sons resemble their father more than a raven resembles a raven."

Sūrat al-Batta [i.e., the sūra of decisive/final divorce — a name for this passage]. And she wished to return to her first husband, so the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, refused her; then she came in Abū Bakr's time, and Abū Bakr refused her; then she came in ʿUmar's time, and ʿUmar said: "I witnessed your love for the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, and for Abū Bakr — and if you seek to return to your first husband, I will surely stone you."

"**So if he divorces her**" — the second husband — "**there is no blame upon either of them**" — the wife and the first husband — "**that they return to one another**" — to marriage — "**if they think that they will be able to uphold the limits of God. And these**" — the aforementioned [rulings] — "**are the limits of God, which He makes clear to a people who know. And when you divorce women and they reach their term**" — near the end of their waiting period — "**then either retain them**" — by taking them back before the waiting period ends — "**in a fitting manner, or release them**

**[196]** **in a fitting manner**" — leave them until their waiting period ends; once it ends, she is [finally] separated. "**And do not retain them**" — by taking them back — "**to cause harm**" — reconciliation is [permitted] only for the sake of making peace; as for taking them back merely to cause harm — "**thereby transgressing**" against them — by compelling them toward ransom, further divorce, or prolonged confinement — do not do that. "**And whoever does that has wronged himself**" — by exposing himself to God's punishment — "**and do not take God's signs in mockery**" — treat them as an object of mockery through opposing them — "**and remember God's favor upon you**" — through Islam — "**and what He has sent down to you of the Book**" — the Qur'an — "**and wisdom**" — the rulings it contains — "**by which He admonishes you**" — that you might be grateful by acting upon it — "**and fear God, and know that God knows all things**" — nothing is hidden from Him.

"**And when you divorce women**" — occasion of revelation: the sister of Maʿqil ibn Yasār was divorced by her husband, and afterward he sought to take her back, and she was willing, but her brother refused — so this was revealed: "**And when you divorce women and they reach their term, do not prevent them**" — O guardians, do not prevent them — "**from marrying their [former] husbands**" — who had divorced them —

**[197]** "**when they agree between themselves in a fitting manner**" — lawfully. "**That**" — the prohibition — "**is enjoined upon whoever among you believes in God and the Last Day**" — for it is he who benefits by it — "**that is purer**" — better — "**for you, and more wholesome**" for you and for them, given what is feared for the couple from suspicion arising from their relationship. "**And God knows**" what is beneficial, "**while you do not know**" — so follow His command.

"**Mothers shall nurse their children for two full years**" — a confirming description — "**for whoever wishes to complete the nursing**" — and no more is required beyond that — "**and it is upon the father**" — the one to whom the child was born — "**to provide their sustenance**" — feeding the mothers — "**and their clothing**" — for the nursing, if they have been divorced — "**in accordance with what is fitting**" — according to his capacity — "**no soul is charged beyond its capacity**" — its ability — "**no mother shall be made to suffer harm on account of her child**" — by being compelled to nurse him against her will — "**nor shall a father [suffer harm] on account of his child**" — by being charged beyond his capacity — the ascription of the child to each of them in both places is meant to evoke tenderness. "**And upon the heir**" — the father's heir, that is, the child — "**a similar duty**" — to what was upon the father, of sustenance and clothing for the mother. "**And if the two of them desire weaning**" — before the two years, "**by mutual consent and consultation**" between them, so that the child's interest is served, "**there is no blame upon them**" in that. "**And if you wish to have your children nursed [by others]**" — wet-nurses other than the mothers — "**there is no blame upon you**" in that, "**provided you hand over what you have agreed to give**" of the wage, "**in a fitting manner**" — with graciousness of spirit — "**and fear God, and know that God sees what you do**" — nothing of it is hidden from Him.

"**Those among you who die and leave wives**" — "**shall bequeath**" — "**for their wives**

... [see continuation note below]

**[197/198]** ... "**a waiting period of four months and ten [nights]**" — this applies to non-pregnant women; their waiting period, if pregnant, is [governed] by the divorce verse [i.e., until delivery], and the slave-woman's period is half of that, by the Sunna. "**And when they reach their term**" — their waiting period ends — "**there is no blame upon you for what they do concerning themselves**" — of adorning themselves and presenting themselves to suitors — "**in a fitting manner**" — lawfully — "**and God is aware of what you do**" — knowing the inward as He knows the outward. "**And there is no blame upon you for what you hint at**" — of proposing marriage to women whose husbands have died, while they are still in their waiting period — such as saying, for instance, "You are indeed beautiful, and who would not want someone like you — there is many a suitor interested in you" — "**or [what you] conceal within yourselves**" — of the intention to marry them — "**God knows that you will be mentioning them [to yourselves]**" through proposing, and that you will not be able to keep from them, so He permitted you the hint — "**but do not make secret promises with them**" — of marriage — "**except that you speak in a fitting way**" — what is legally recognized as a "hint," which is permitted to you. "**And do not resolve on the marriage contract**" — to conclude it — "**until the prescribed [period]**" — of the waiting period — "**reaches its term**" — comes to its end — "**and know that God knows what is in your souls**" — of resolve and otherwise — "**so beware of Him**" — lest He punish you — "**and know that God is Forgiving**" toward whoever is wary of Him, "**Forbearing**" in delaying punishment from those who deserve it. This is the waiting period for widowhood: four months and ten nights — it abrogated "**provision for a year**" [the earlier ruling]. The waiting period had originally been a full year, then the verse preceding it in [textual] order abrogated it — this is part of the wisdom of the Qur'an. And the reason [for the specific duration of four months and ten days] is that God created Adam — forty years [in symbolic terms] as sticky clay, forty years from molded mud, forty as [fired] pottery — that makes forty, three times, and then the spirit was breathed into him. This is the origin of Adam's creation: when God commanded the spirit to enter Adam's body, the spirit was reluctant, until the Lord said to it: Enter unwillingly, and you shall depart unwillingly. So the spirit entered, and when it reached the nose, Adam sneezed and said: Praise be to God — he was the first to utter this phrase, so the angels blessed him [with "may God have mercy on you"] and said: For this you were created.

**[198]** Then when the spirit passed on until it reached his knees, he wished to stand — hence the Real addressed him: "**Man was created hasty**" — so the children of Adam have never ceased to be hasty. When the spirit filled him completely he rose and walked, and became a living being after having been inanimate matter.

The first to say "Glory be to God" were the angels who bear the Throne; the first to say "Praise be to God" was Adam, peace be upon him; the first to say "There is no god but God" was Noah, peace be upon him; the first to say "God is greater" was Abraham — these are the "enduring good words" — and the first to say "There is no power and no strength save in God, the Exalted, the Tremendous" was Muḥammad, the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him. The origin of Adam's creation was forty years, three times over; hence the child of Adam is created in his mother's womb: forty days a drop (*nuṭfa*), forty days a clot (*ʿalaqa*), forty days a lump of flesh (*muḍgha*), and then the spirit is breathed into him — forty, three times, [totaling] four months. And the waiting period was legislated to safeguard lineages, so that they not become confused: if a man dies, the woman waits four months; if she is pregnant, the pregnancy must become evident, since the spirit is [then] breathed into [the fetus] — and the spirit, when it settles, may at first be faint, but after ten additional days it must move markedly —

**[199]** hence ten days are added to the four months, and this is the waiting period of the widow, unless she is pregnant — if she delivers her pregnancy, her womb is [thereby] cleared [of any doubt about lineage].

"**And there is no blame upon you if you divorce women whom you have not touched**" — that is, had intercourse with — "**or have not yet obligated a dower for**" — divorce before consummation carries no blame. "**And provide for them**" — give them something to benefit from — "**the wealthy according to his means, and the straitened according to his means**" — this indicates that regard is had not to the wife's standing but to the husband's condition alone — "**a provision**" — a benefit — "**in a fitting manner**" — lawfully — "**a duty upon those who do good**." The wisdom in this provision is that divorce wounds the woman's feelings, and whoever wounds a Muslim's feelings ought to make amends — hence this provision was legislated after divorce. "**And if you divorce them before you have touched them, but you had specified a dower for them, then [give] half of what you specified**" — if the dower had been fixed and the divorce occurs before consummation, then half the dower is the woman's and half remains with the husband — "**unless**" — "**they [the women] forgo it**" — [it is otherwise] unless the wife forgoes her claim: since the marriage contract was concluded and nothing passed between us, let him keep his wealth — "**or the one in whose hand is the marriage tie forgoes it**" — or the husband forgoes his claim — either the woman relents or the man relents — "**and that you forgo is nearer to piety**" — and the man's forgoing is more excellent, since the man is stronger and more complete — "**and do not forget graciousness between yourselves**" — in any case, let each of you be gracious to the other, whether man or woman — "**indeed God sees what you do**," and so will requite you for it.

"**Guard the prayers**" — the five, performing them in their times — "**and the middle prayer**" — this is the afternoon prayer (ʿaṣr), or

**[200]** the dawn prayer, or the noon prayer, or others — [there are] various opinions. God obligated prayer upon this community, and the obligatory prayers are five in the day and night — several Qur'anic verses indicate this, among them this one: He said "**Guard the prayers**" — "prayers" is a plural, and the least of a plural [in Arabic usage in some contexts] is three — "**and the middle prayer**," conjoined to it — the middle prayer is disputed, and from this [conjunction] the five prayers are derived. "**And stand for God, devoutly obedient**" — stand before God in prayer, devoutly obedient — some say [this means] compliant, others say silent: they used to speak during prayer in the early period of Islam, and when this verse was revealed they were commanded to silence during prayer.

The Messenger said: "There is preoccupation enough in prayer [to forbid speech]." And the five prayers are expiatory: the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: If there were a river at any of your doors in which he bathed five times each day, would anything remain of his filth? They said: No, O Messenger of God. He said: So it is with the five prayers — "the five prayers are an expiation for what lies between them." The Real, Blessed and Exalted, addressed Moses, saying to him: I shall grant Muḥammad and his community a prayer of four cycles — the noon prayer. In

**[201]** the first cycle I forgive them all their sins; in the second, the angels seek forgiveness for them, and whoever the angels seek forgiveness for, I do not punish; in the third, the gates of heaven are opened for them and their supplication is answered; and in the fourth, My good pleasure descends upon them, so that I am never wrathful with them again.

"**But if you fear**" — an enemy, a flood, or a predator — "**then [pray] on foot**" — the plural of "walking [on foot]," that is, pedestrians — "**or riding**" — the plural of "mounted" — this is the fear-prayer; it is permitted for the fearful to pray while riding or while walking, and even speaking [if necessary]. "**And when you are safe**" from fear, "**remember God**" — pray — "**as He has taught you what you did not know**" — before He taught you its obligations and its rights. The middle prayer is the most excellent of the five prayers — it was concealed so that you would guard all five prayers, just as the Night of Power was concealed within this blessed month so that you would revive all the nights of Ramaḍān, and just as the [hidden] saintly individual is concealed among people so that you would respect all Muslims, and just as the hour of [answered] supplication on Friday is concealed, and just as the Greatest Name is concealed among [God's] Names, and just as the accepted act of worship is concealed among all acts of worship — so that the servant would be eager for every good thing. It has been related that it is the dawn prayer — this is Mālik's position, on account of its being burdensome upon souls; and it has been said that it is the afternoon prayer, and this is the stronger [view],

**[202]** on account of the ḥadīths transmitted supporting it.

[Note: "سورة البقرة" reappears here in the source as a running page-header — this is still within al-Baqara (the Ṭālūt/Jālūt pericope, vv. 246–252), not a move to a new sūra.]

"**Thus**" — as He has made clear what has been mentioned — "**does God make His signs clear to you, that you may understand**" — reflect.

"**Have you not considered**" — an expression of astonishment, inviting attention to what follows — has your knowledge not reached those who — "**went forth from their homes, and they were thousands**" — four thousand, or eight thousand, or ten thousand, or forty thousand, or seventy thousand — "**for fear of death**" — a plague had struck their land, so they fled in flight from death; they were a people of the Children of Israel, the people of Ḥizqīl [Ezekiel], a prophet of God — "**and God said to them, 'Die,'**" so when they had fled and grown distant from their lands by eight days, it was said to them, "Die," and they all died — "**then He gave them life**" — after eight days, through the supplication of their prophet Ḥizqīl; Ḥizqīl continued to entreat and supplicate God until they were resurrected, and lived on for an age, bearing upon them the mark of death — they could find no garment that did not turn to a shroud; the foul odor one still smells on the Jews to this day is from that death — "**Indeed God is possessed of favor toward mankind**" — including His reviving of these — "**but most people**" — the disbelievers — "**do not give thanks**." And the purpose of relating this account is to encourage the believers toward fighting.

God wishes to command us to jihad, while the soul fears death, so He related this story: those who fled from death, when God said to them "Die," they all died — so [it follows that] whether we advance to jihad or hold back, it makes no difference once the time of death has come.

"**And fight in the path of God**" — to exalt His religion — "**and know that God is All-Hearing**"

**[203]** of your words, "**All-Knowing**" of your states, so He will requite you. "**Who is it that will lend God a goodly loan**" — by spending his wealth in the path of God, "**a goodly loan**" — spending it for God's sake, Mighty and Glorious, out of a pure heart — "**that He may multiply it for him many times over**" — from ten up to more than seven hundred [times]. God, Blessed and Exalted, out of the perfection of

**[204]** His favor and His concern for the poor, named charity given for God's sake a "loan." And why did He call it a loan? Because if a person asks you for money and says "give me" or "grant me," you do not expect anything back from him; but if he says "lend me," you expect him to return what he took. Hence the servant becomes assured that God will return to him the reward of his charity, in that He called it a loan — and God, out of the perfection of His tenderness toward the poor, placed Himself in the position of the poor person. It is related in a sound ḥadīth that the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, says: "God will stand a person before Him on the Day of Resurrection and say: O My servant, I fell ill and you did not visit Me — this is a great mystery — O My servant, I asked you for food and you did not feed Me; O My servant, I asked you for drink and you did not give Me drink. He will say: How could I visit You, when You are the Lord of the Worlds? He will say: Did you not know that My servant so-and-so fell ill, and had you visited him you would have found that [reward] with Me? How could I feed You, my Lord, when You are the Lord of the Worlds? He will say: Did you not know that My servant so-and-so asked you for food, and had you fed him you would have found that with Me? How could I give You drink, my Lord? He will say: My servant so-and-so asked you for drink, and had you given him drink you would have found that with Me."

Consider this: he, peace and blessings upon him, says that God, Mighty and Glorious, gives His believing servant, for a single good deed, a thousand thousand good deeds [in reward]. Abū Hurayra said: No, rather I heard the Messenger of God, peace and blessings upon him, say that God, Mighty and Glorious, gives him two thousand thousand good deeds — then he recited "**He multiplies it, and grants from His own presence a magnificent reward**" — and said: once He says "a magnificent reward," who can gauge its measure?

**[205]** The lowly beggar standing at your door asking you for charity — tomorrow he will call you to account [before God] — and the loan carries more reward than the [outright] gift. The Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, on the night of his Ascension, found written on the gate of Paradise: "Charity is [rewarded] tenfold, and the loan eighteenfold." So he said to Gabriel: How strange — charity is given by a person purely for God's sake and does not return to him, while the loan returns to him, and yet it carries greater reward? He said to him: Yes — because a person may be given charity while not truly in need, whereas no one seeks a loan except out of genuine need; whoever borrows, borrows only out of need — hence the reward of the loan is greater with God than the reward of charity. And this community, [the verse] "**Whoever comes with a good deed shall have ten times its like**" was revealed concerning them, so the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: O Lord, increase my community — so the verse of charity was revealed (it will come tomorrow, God willing): "**The likeness of those who spend their wealth in the path of God is as the likeness of a grain that sprouts seven ears**" — a portion multiplied to seven hundred — so the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: O Lord, increase my community — so this verse was revealed: "**Who is it that will lend God a goodly loan, that He may multiply it for him many times over**" — up to more than seven hundred — the Messenger, peace and blessings upon him, said: O Lord, increase my community — so it was revealed: "**Only the patient shall be paid their reward in full, without reckoning**" — so he said: I am content with God as Lord. And God, Blessed and Exalted, was

**[206]** content with the Chosen One, peace and blessings upon him.

"**And God withholds**" — restrains provision from whom He wills, as a trial — "**and extends [it]**" — expands it for whom He wills, as a test — "**and unto Him you shall be returned**" — in the hereafter, through resurrection.

"**Have you not considered the assembly of the Children of Israel after Moses**" — their story — "**when they said to a prophet of theirs**" — he was Samuel — "**'Raise up for us a king, that we may fight**" alongside him "**in the path of God'? He said, 'Might it be that if fighting is prescribed for you, you would not fight?'**" — perhaps, if jihad were made obligatory upon you, you would not accept it — "**They said, 'And why should we not fight in the path of God, when we have been driven from our homes and our children?**'" — on account of them, this had been done to them by the people of Goliath — "**But when fighting was prescribed for them, they turned away**" — and were cowardly — "**except a few of them**" — those who [later] crossed the river with Ṭālūt (Saul), as will come — "**and God knows the wrongdoers**" — and will requite them. And the prophet asked his Lord to send a king, and He answered him by sending Ṭālūt.

"**And their prophet said to them, 'Indeed God has raised up for you**

**[207]** **Ṭālūt as king'**" — God made Ṭālūt king over you — "**They said, 'How can he have kingship over us, when we have greater right to kingship than he,**'" — the custom of the children of Adam — this one does not deserve kingship over us, he is not of the tribe of kingship nor of the tribe of prophecy, he was a tanner or a shepherd, not of the tribe of Judah nor of the tribe of Reuben — the tribe of Judah being the people of prophecy and the tribe of Reuben the people of kingship — and this man was of the tribe of Benjamin, and they do not deserve kingship over us — "**'and he has not been given ample wealth**'" — [he is] a poor man with nothing, how can he manage to establish a kingdom? "**He said**" — their prophet said to them — "**'Indeed God has chosen him**" — selected him for kingship — "**over you, and increased him amply**" — abundantly — "**in knowledge and physique**'" — for he was, at that time, the most learned of the Children of Israel, the most handsome, and the most perfectly formed of them — when this verse was revealed concerning him he became the most learned of them and the finest of them in physique — "**'and God gives His kingship to whom He wills**'" — and despite this there is no objection to God, Blessed and Exalted, giving His kingship to whomever He wills — "**'and God is All-Encompassing**" in His favor, "**All-Knowing**'" of who is worthy of it.

"**And their prophet said to them**" — they asked him for a sign of his kingship — "**'Indeed the sign of his kingship is that the Ark shall come to you**'" — the chest: the prophets had possessed the ark of Adam, which had come down with him from heaven, containing images of all the prophets, and it had reached

**[208]** Moses. Whenever they fought, God gave them victory by virtue of this ark; but when the Amalekites overcame them, they took the ark from them; [the Israelites] used to seek aid through it and thereby overcome their enemy. The Exalted said, "**'in it is tranquility**'" — reassurance for your hearts — "**'from your Lord, and a remnant of what the family of Moses**'" — meaning Moses — "**'and the family of Aaron**'" — meaning Aaron — "**'left behind**'" — the two of them had left it for [the ark], and in it were Moses's two sandals and his staff, Aaron's turban, a measure of the manna that used to descend upon them, and fragments of the Tablets that had broken when Moses cast them down — the remainder of them was in the ark — "**'the angels bearing it**'" — if you see the angels carrying this ark until they set it down before Ṭālūt, that is the sign of his kingship — "**'Indeed in that is surely a sign for you, if you are believers**'" — so the angels carried it between heaven and earth while [the people] watched, until they set it down beside Ṭālūt, and so they acknowledged his kingship and hastened to jihad; and he chose from among their young men seventy thousand.

"**And when Ṭālūt set out**" — departed — "**with the troops**" from Jerusalem — the weather was extremely hot, and they asked him for water — "**he said, 'Indeed God will test you**" — put you to trial — "**with a river**'" — so as to distinguish the obedient among you from the disobedient; it lay between Jordan and Palestine — "**'so whoever drinks from it is not of me**'" — of my followers — "**'and whoever does not taste it**'" — does not drink of it — "**'he is indeed of me — except one who takes a scoop with his hand**'" — content with that alone, without exceeding it — "**'he is of me**.'" "**But they drank of it**" — when they reached the river most of them drank abundantly — "**except a few**

**[209]** **of them**" — who confined themselves to a scoop; it is related that this sufficed them and their mounts, and they numbered three hundred and some — the number of the People of Badr, and the number of the [earlier] messengers. "**And when he had crossed it, he and those who believed with him**" — these were the ones who

said, "**'We have no strength today against Goliath and his troops**'" — [that is, others] refused to fight them and grew cowardly and did not cross with him — "**Those who were certain**" — convinced — "**that they would meet God**" — through resurrection, and these were the ones who had confined themselves to the scoop — "**said, 'How often has a small company overcome a large company by God's leave**'" — by His will — "**'and God is with the patient**'" — with support and victory. "**And when they came out into the open against Goliath and his troops**" — appeared for battle and drew up in ranks — "**they said, 'Our Lord, pour out upon us patience**'" — bestow — "**'and make firm our feet**'" — by strengthening our hearts for the struggle — "**'and grant us victory over the disbelieving people**'" — "**So they defeated them by God's leave**" — by His will — "**and David killed**" — and he was in Ṭālūt's army — "**Goliath**" — the giant — "**and God gave him**" — [David] — "**kingship**" — over the Children of Israel — "**and wisdom**" — prophecy and kingship had never before been combined in any one person among the Children of Israel, until they were united in David, peace be upon him; he became king and prophet after the deaths of Samuel and Ṭālūt, inheriting both of their offices — "**and taught him of what He willed**" — such as the craft of making chain-mail — "**And were it not for God's repelling some people by means of others, the earth would have been corrupted**" — were it not that God repels calamity from some by means of others, the earth would have been corrupted through the domination of the polytheists and the killing of the Muslims — "**but God is possessed of favor toward the worlds**" — repelling some by means of others. "**These**" — these verses — "**are the signs of God, which We recite**" — narrate — "**to you**," O Muḥammad, "**in truth, and indeed you are among the messengers**."
